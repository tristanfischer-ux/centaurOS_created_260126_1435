# Atlantic Salmon Post-Smolt Land-Based RAS — Tayinloan (£5,000,000 capex ceiling, continuous monthly-cohort post-smolt production)

We are designing a land-based, recirculating aquaculture system (RAS) that grows Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) from 100 gram seawater-ready smolts to 1 kilogram post-smolts, on the same consented brownfield former flow-through fish-farm site at Tayinloan, Kintyre, Argyll, Scotland. The product is not a finished food fish: it is a robust 1 kilogram post-smolt sold under contract to marine grow-out farms, which removes the salmon industry's riskiest early sea phase from the buyer and lets them stock larger, hardier fish for a shorter, lower-mortality sea cycle. A RAS is a continuously-operated water-treatment and life-support plant wrapped around circular fish-rearing tanks; the engineering problem is holding dissolved oxygen, total ammonia nitrogen, nitrite, carbon dioxide, suspended solids, pH, alkalinity, salinity and pathogen pressure inside safe limits while the fish are fed heavily every day. The plant recirculates approximately 99.5 percent of its water (about 0.5 percent new-water make-up).

The decisive difference from a warm-water RAS is temperature. Atlantic salmon post-smolts grow optimally at 12 to 14 degrees Celsius, and the raw seawater at this Scottish site is approximately 12 degrees Celsius. The plant therefore runs at AMBIENT temperature with NO bulk heating requirement — at most a small trim to hold 13 degrees Celsius. This eliminates the single largest operating cost and the single largest fixed capital item of a warm-water RAS: there are no heat pumps, no immersion heaters, no make-up-water heating duty, and no requirement for a heavily insulated, heated building envelope. The design must exploit this: tanks are insulated and covered to hold ambient water temperature and exclude light and pathogens, housed under a light weather-and-biosecurity cover rather than a heated hall, with only a focused, heated, food-grade handling and harvest building.

Capex ceiling: 5,000,000 pounds for the recirculating aquaculture system equipment, shipping and installation, on the existing brownfield site. This dossier realises the largest internally-consistent post-smolt output whose installed cost does not exceed the 5,000,000 pound ceiling. Because there is no thermal plant and no heated building, a far larger share of the capital buys productive capacity (rearing tanks, water treatment, oxygenation) than in a warm-water plant, so the realised output should substantially exceed the tonnage a heated tropical-species plant achieves at the same ceiling.

Production model — CONTINUOUS MONTHLY-COHORT SCHEDULE (the core sizing driver):
- The plant runs a continuous, rolling production schedule. A fixed cohort of seawater-ready smolts is stocked EVERY MONTH, and a cohort of 1 kilogram post-smolts is harvested EVERY MONTH, giving a steady monthly output and a steady standing biomass.
- The smolt-to-post-smolt grow-out cycle is approximately 8 months (240 days) at 13 degrees Celsius, from 100 grams to 1,000 grams. With monthly stocking and an 8-month cycle, approximately 8 cohorts are present in the system at any time, each at a different size.
- The fish therefore progress through a CASCADE of tank SIZE-CLASSES as they grow: small, high-density tanks for the 100 gram entry fish, intermediate tanks for the mid-grow stages, and large tanks for the 1 kilogram post-smolts. Each size-class holds the cohorts in its weight band; fish are graded and moved up the cascade as they grow. The monthly cohort size, the cohort progression, the number and volume of tanks in EACH size-class, and the resulting standing biomass are the primary quantities the design must derive — this is fundamentally a batch-scheduling-and-tankage problem, not a single uniform grow-out.
- Indicative target: a monthly cohort of approximately 10,000 smolts, giving approximately 120,000 post-smolts per year (approximately 120 tonnes per year of 1 kilogram post-smolts), subject to the 5,000,000 pound ceiling. The standing biomass is the sum across the 8 in-system cohorts of cohort-number times mean-cohort-weight.
- Fish are moved between size-class tanks HYDRAULICALLY: cohorts are crowded with in-tank crowder screens and transferred by fish pumps through pipework to an in-line grader and on to the next size-class — fish are never netted or lifted from above. This is what makes covered, lidded tanks operable.

Per-fish biological parameters:
- Smolt stocking weight 100 grams; post-smolt harvest weight 1,000 grams; grow-out cycle approximately 240 days at 13 degrees Celsius.
- Feed conversion ratio approximately 1.15 (salmon convert more efficiently than warm-water carnivores).
- Peak stocking density up to 75 kilograms per cubic metre in the large post-smolt size-class; lower in the smaller-fish size-classes (progressive density up the cascade).
- Tank volume is derived from the per-size-class standing biomass and the size-class density, NOT a single total — expect a cascade of differently-sized tanks, with many smaller tanks at the entry end.

Process description (continuous recirculating loop, in flow order — applied per size-class loop or shared treatment as the design determines):
- Rear the salmon in circular, self-cleaning, COVERED tanks with dual Cornell-type drains: a small bottom-centre solids-rich draw and a larger elevated side-wall draw; tangential inlet jets generate rotational tea-cup flow that sweeps solids to the centre. Tanks are sized in a cascade of size-classes, smallest at the smolt-entry end.
- Remove settleable and suspended solids on tank exit in rotary drum microscreen filters with a 40 to 60 micron screen and automatic backwash.
- Oxidise total ammonia nitrogen to nitrate in moving-bed biofilm reactor (MBBR) biofilters carrying high-specific-surface plastic media, sized on the daily feed load, holding total ammonia nitrogen below 2 milligrams per litre and nitrite low.
- Strip carbon dioxide to below 15 milligrams per litre in forced-draught counter-current packed-column degassers (salmon post-smolts tolerate higher CO2 than warm-water sashimi species, but it must still be controlled).
- Re-oxygenate with pure oxygen through down-flow bubble contactors (oxygen cones) fed from on-site liquid oxygen and a pressure-swing-adsorption oxygen generator, sustaining dissolved oxygen at or above saturation at the tank inlet.
- Disinfect and polish the recirculating water with ultraviolet reactors at a validated dose to control free-floating pathogens.
- Hold temperature at AMBIENT (approximately 12 to 13 degrees Celsius) — NO bulk heating plant; recover heat passively only. Dose sodium bicarbonate to replace alkalinity consumed by nitrification and hold pH at 7.0 to 7.4.
- Return the treated, oxygenated water to the tanks through the tangential inlets; bleed the approximately 0.5 percent make-up, and thicken, dewater and dispose of the concentrated solids and sludge stream.

Water-quality set points (Atlantic salmon post-smolt): temperature 13 degrees Celsius (ambient, no heating), pH 7.2, salinity approximately 30 parts per thousand (post-smolts are seawater-adapted; the site's beach wells and boreholes allow salinity tuning), total ammonia nitrogen below 2 milligrams per litre, nitrite low, carbon dioxide below 15 milligrams per litre, dissolved oxygen at or above saturation at the tank inlet.

Target market: 1 kilogram Atlantic salmon post-smolts sold under a committed annual supply contract to Scottish marine salmon grow-out farms, as a de-risked, biosecure, land-based input to the sea-cage cycle. This is a large, established, guaranteed domestic market — the Scottish salmon industry stocks tens of millions of smolts and post-smolts per year and is actively moving to larger post-smolts to shorten the sea phase. Site: Tayinloan, Argyll, Scotland; an 18-acre consented brownfield former flow-through farm with seawater beach wells and freshwater boreholes giving roughly 124 cubic metres per hour of sustainable make-up water, four existing sea outfall pipes extending about 170 metres into the Sound of Gigha, and a planned 5 megavolt-ampere, 6 megawatt renewable micro-grid; United Kingdom deployment under Marine Scotland consent.

Life-support and backup (mission-critical: at density the fish can die within about 20 minutes of an oxygen or flow failure): duty and standby recirculation pumps with gravity-flow fall-back, emergency pure-oxygen solenoid diffusers in every tank that fail open on power loss, a backup electrical generator sized to the CRITICAL life-support load (recirculation, oxygenation and controls) rather than the whole plant, a liquid-oxygen buffer sized for several hours, and a fail-safe control system with low-dissolved-oxygen, high-and-low water-level, pump-trip and oxygen-pressure alarms with auto-dialler escalation. Because there is no heating plant, the critical electrical load and therefore the standby generator are markedly smaller than in a warm-water RAS.
