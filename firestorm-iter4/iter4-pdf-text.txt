Overview
This engineering decomposition details an 8 kW residential air-source heat pump (ASHP) optimised for
the UK market, utilizing low-GWP R290 (propane) refrigerant. Designed to integrate seamlessly with
standard 200 L domestic hot water cylinders in semi-detached homes, the unit balances high seasonal
efficiency (SCOP 3.5) with strict cost controls to meet a £1,800 OEM unit cost ceiling. The architecture
relies on a 230 V single-phase supply, making it universally compatible with UK residential electrical
infrastructure. To safely handle the A3 flammable refrigerant, the design incorporates stringent ignition-source isolation, including sealed relays, remote-mounted contactors, and a dedicated R290 leak
detector positioned at the compartment's high point. The acoustic profile is heavily attenuated using
specialised compressor jackets and optimised axial fan aerodynamics to ensure compliance with the
MCS 020 sound power limit of 56 dBA at 1 metre, crucial for UK planning permission exemptions.

Mission
To deliver a highly efficient, cost-optimised, and fully compliant R290 air-source heat pump tailored for
UK semi-detached homes.

Target customers
UK semi-detached homes requiring hot water tank integration (200L cylinder). Installers operating under the MCS scheme.

Risk & Failure-Mode Analysis
Class-level pre-mitigation hazards a heat pump design must address. Each hazard is rated on three 1-5
scales whose product gives a single risk priority number — higher means worse before mitigation.
Severity — how bad the outcome is if the hazard occurs (1 = inconvenience, 5 = injury / fire / total loss).
Likelihood — how often it tends to happen in fielded systems before mitigation (1 = very rare, 5 = frequent).
Detectability — how hard it is to spot before it causes harm (1 = obvious / instrumented, 5 = silent failure).
Risk priority — severity × likelihood × detectability. The single number used to rank hazards.

Heat pump hazards centre on refrigerant containment (especially A3-class refrigerants like R290 propane),
refrigerant-circuit pressure containment, and rotating-machinery safety. Residential R290 systems balance
flammability risk against the F-gas regulatory pressure to phase out high-GWP HFCs.

Pinhole leak at brazed joint, capillary fatigue, or service-valve seat degradation releases propane; mixes
with air to flammable concentration in confined spaces; ignites at ~510 °C or by spark.
Typical mitigations: minimise refrigerant charge per BS EN 378 charge-limit tables for room volume;

refrigerant circuit envelope.
Detection: catalytic-bead or IR refrigerant leak sensors at 25 % LEL; pressure-drop trending in service
intervals.
