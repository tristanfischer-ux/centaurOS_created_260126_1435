# Content & Marketing Frameworks

Apply proven frameworks from marketing legends when creating content, copy, offers, or go-to-market strategy.

## Discovery (Before You Start)

Before applying any framework, ensure you have answers to:

- [ ] **What's the goal?** (Awareness, leads, conversion, retention, referrals)
- [ ] **Who's the audience?** (Demographics, psychographics, pain points)
- [ ] **What awareness level is the audience at?** (Unaware → Most Aware — see Schwartz framework below)
- [ ] **What's the channel?** (Social, email, landing page, ads, pitch, sales call)
- [ ] **What's the company stage?** (Pre-revenue, growth, scaling — determines which frameworks apply)
- [ ] **What proof exists?** (Results, testimonials, case studies, metrics)

## Core Frameworks

### 1. Schwartz Awareness Levels (Audience Targeting)

**When to use:** Before writing ANY copy, content, or messaging. This is the FIRST decision.

| Level | Audience State | What They Need | Example Headline |
|-------|---------------|----------------|------------------|
| **Unaware** | Don't know they have a problem | Stories that make the problem visible | "The hidden cost of [thing they do daily]" |
| **Problem Aware** | Know the problem, don't know solutions exist | Agitation + solution category intro | "Tired of [problem]? There's a better way." |
| **Solution Aware** | Know solutions exist, don't know YOUR solution | Differentiation, mechanism, proof | "Why [your approach] works when [alternatives] don't" |
| **Product Aware** | Know your product, haven't bought | Social proof, urgency, risk reversal | "[X] companies switched this month. Here's why." |
| **Most Aware** | Past customers, warm leads | New offers, upgrades, referral incentives | "Exclusive for members: [new offer]" |

**Decision rule:** Always identify the awareness level FIRST, then match all messaging to that level.

**Anti-pattern:** Writing Product Aware copy (features, pricing, urgency) for an Unaware audience. They'll ignore it completely.

### 2. Hormozi Value Equation (Offer Design)

**When to use:** When designing offers, pricing, landing pages, or sales proposals.

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

**To increase value:**
- **Increase Dream Outcome:** Make the result more specific and more desirable
- **Increase Perceived Likelihood:** Add proof, guarantees, case studies
- **Decrease Time Delay:** Promise faster results, quick wins, immediate access
- **Decrease Effort & Sacrifice:** Done-for-you, templates, systems, automation

**Value Stack Pattern:**
| Component | Purpose | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Core Offer | Solves the main problem | The course/product/service |
| Bonus 1 | Accelerates results | Quick-start templates |
| Bonus 2 | Removes obstacles | Done-for-you setup |
| Bonus 3 | Eliminates sacrifice | Community access |
| Fast-Action Bonus | Creates urgency | Extra 1:1 session for first 10 |

**Anti-pattern:** Listing features instead of mapping to the value equation. Features don't sell — dream outcomes sell.

### 3. Gary Vee Content Pyramid (Content Volume)

**When to use:** When creating a content strategy or repurposing a single piece.

```
1 Pillar Content Piece
    → 5-7 Quote Cards
    → 3-5 Video Clips
    → 10-15 Text Posts
    → 1-2 Carousels/Threads
    → 1 Blog/Newsletter
    → 5-10 Story Slides
```

**Key principles:**
- Volume beats perfection when each piece is platform-native
- Document, don't create (real > polished)
- Each micro piece must stand alone as valuable
- Never cross-post — always platform-native

**Anti-pattern:** Creating one piece and posting it identically across all platforms. A LinkedIn post is not a tweet is not an Instagram caption.

### 4. Brunson Story Framework (Narrative Selling)

**When to use:** Pitch decks, brand stories, case studies, sales pages, email sequences.

```
Character → Desire → Wall → Epiphany → Plan → Conflict → Achievement → Transformation
```

| Beat | Purpose | Emotional Effect |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| **Character** | Who is the hero? Make them relatable | Connection |
| **Desire** | What do they want? Make the goal clear | Aspiration |
| **Wall** | What blocked them? The obstacle | Tension |
| **Epiphany** | The breakthrough insight | "Aha!" |
| **Plan** | How they'll win | Confidence |
| **Conflict** | What challenges remain | Realism |
| **Achievement** | What they've proven | Trust |
| **Transformation** | What the future holds | Excitement |

**Key insight:** The Wall → Epiphany transition is the emotional climax. If this moment doesn't land, the whole story falls flat.

**Anti-pattern:** Skipping the Wall. Without struggle, the achievement feels unearned and the story is forgettable.

### 5. Kennedy Direct Response Principles (Conversion Copy)

**When to use:** Any copy that needs to convert — ads, emails, landing pages, outreach, proposals.

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Specificity over vagueness** | "47% improvement" beats "significant improvement" |
| **Reason-why advertising** | Always explain WHY (why this price, why this deadline, why this offer) |
| **Deadline-driven urgency** | Real deadlines with real reasons outperform open-ended offers |
| **Risk reversal guarantees** | The more specific the guarantee, the fewer people claim it |
| **Social proof stacking** | Stack multiple types: numbers, testimonials, logos, case studies |

**Anti-pattern:** Using urgency without a reason-why. "Offer ends Friday!" without explaining WHY it ends Friday feels fake.

### 6. Deiss Customer Value Optimization (Growth Strategy)

**When to use:** When designing growth strategy, pricing tiers, or customer journey.

```
Awareness → Engagement → Subscribe → Convert → Excite → Ascend → Advocate → Promote
```

**Key principle:** A 10% improvement at EVERY stage compounds into massive total growth. Don't optimize one stage — optimize all of them.

**Value Ladder:**
```
Free (Lead Magnet) → Low-Ticket (£1-50) → Core (£100-1K) → Premium (£1K-10K) → High-Ticket (£10K+)
```

**Anti-pattern:** Obsessing over customer acquisition while ignoring customer value. Acquiring a customer for £50 who spends £100 total is worse than acquiring one for £100 who spends £5,000 over their lifetime.

## Viral Content Formulas

### Hormozi Thread Formula
```
Tweet 1: "I [impressive result] in [timeframe]. Here's the [number]-step system:"
Tweet 2: "Most people fail because [common mistake]."
Tweets 3-10: "Step X: [Specific tactic]" (one per tweet, actionable)
Tweet 11: "The difference between [beginners] and [experts]:"
Tweet 12: "Repost if this was valuable. Follow @[you] for more."
```

### Kennedy Urgency Framework
```
Deadline: "Closes Friday at midnight" + reason-why
Scarcity: "Only 10 spots available" + reason-why
Bonus stack: "Plus you get X, Y, Z if you join today" + reason-why
Penalty: "Price doubles after launch week" + reason-why
```

### Brunson Value Ladder Post
```
"Free: [Lead magnet that solves micro problem]
$X: [Tripwire that extends solution]
$XX: [Core offer that creates transformation]
$XXX: [Premium that accelerates results]
This is how you build a business that actually scales."
```

## Platform-Specific Patterns

| Platform | Format | Hook Style | Length | CTA Style |
|----------|--------|------------|--------|-----------|
| **Twitter/X** | Thread | Bold claim + number | 280 chars/tweet | "Repost + follow" |
| **LinkedIn** | Story post | Personal struggle | 1,300 chars ideal | Question for comments |
| **Instagram** | Carousel | Large text, clean design | 10 slides | "Save this" / "Send to friend" |
| **YouTube** | Long-form | "By end of this video..." | 8-15 min | Mid-roll + end card |
| **Email** | Newsletter | Subject = curiosity | 300-500 words | One clear CTA |

## Rules

- **Always identify awareness level first** — it determines everything
- **Always include specific numbers** — "47% improvement in 30 days" not "significant improvement"
- **Always provide copy-paste-ready output** — not theory, but things the user can use immediately
- **Always match message to channel** — a LinkedIn post is not a tweet is not an email
- **Always include a CTA** — content without a next step is a missed opportunity
- **Always back claims with proof** — or clearly label assumptions

## Anti-Patterns

BAD: Writing features-focused copy for an Unaware audience
WHY: They don't know they have a problem yet. Lead with the problem story first.

BAD: Using fake urgency ("Only 3 left!" when supply is unlimited)
WHY: Destroys trust. Kennedy's principle: urgency with a REASON outperforms urgency without.

BAD: Creating one piece of content and posting it identically everywhere
WHY: Each platform has different algorithms, audiences, and format requirements. Go platform-native.

BAD: Pitching in the first email/post/interaction
WHY: Cold audiences need value first. Lead with a gift, not an ask.

BAD: Vague copy ("We might be able to help you improve your business")
WHY: Specificity converts. "We helped 47 SaaS companies increase trial-to-paid by 34% in 90 days" converts.

## Evaluation (Before Completing)

- [ ] Did I identify the audience's awareness level before writing copy?
- [ ] Does every piece include specific numbers and measurable claims?
- [ ] Is every offer structured using the value equation (dream outcome / effort)?
- [ ] Does every content piece have a clear CTA?
- [ ] Is the output platform-native (not cross-posted)?
- [ ] Are urgency mechanisms genuine with reason-why explanations?
- [ ] Would the user's audience actually engage with this content?
- [ ] Is the copy specific enough that a competitor couldn't use it unchanged?
