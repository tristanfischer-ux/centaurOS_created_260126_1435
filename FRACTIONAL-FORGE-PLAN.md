## 1. What it is

**Fractional Forge is "AWS for hardware founders": a non-engineer founder takes an idea from hunch → a credible concept (engineering *and* business case) → expert-vetted design + business plan → funded, staffed and built company, at a fraction of the time, cost and people — keeping 100% of their IP, equity and control.**

It is a **hardtech origination + capital business** — not a report shop, not an accelerator, not a studio. The AI dossier + expert vetting are the cheap, scalable **de-risking / origination funnel**; the margin and venture upside live **downstream** (a cut of the raise + a small equity kicker + build coordination), powered by the forge-capital investor database. It also **stands up the founder's initial team** (recruitment, in partnership with **Nicholas Ball**) — the AWS analogy extended from infrastructure to *people*, making the "fraction of the people" literal.

The category, precisely: **a fractional deep-tech commercialisation firm.** Studios co-found and take 30–50%; accelerators (EF, HAX) take ~8–10% and run cohorts. FF takes **nothing up front and lets the founder keep their cap table — it wins only when the founder does.** That inversion is the wedge.

---

## 2. The model in one line

Cheap AI de-risking gets a founder to "investable" in days for cash; FF monetises the upside it unlocks (funding + build) while the founder keeps control. The **moat** is the **investor-data-loop** (forge-capital, fed by every dossier) + the **founder-keeps-equity inversion** — *not* the engineering tech, which is replicable.

## 3. The product: the staged journey

Every stage is **walk-away-clean** (EF's "you owe us nothing" principle): the founder owns everything at every step, and FF disengaging never strands them. **Two generated reports, not one:** the **Engineering Dossier** (does it work + how to build it) and a **separate Business/Market Report** (market, customers, go-to-market, business plan — a distinct build, see §9). Both use the same free/£1k/£5k tiering and both feed a **third artifact — the investor Pitch Deck**, generated at the funding stage (§3b); investors fund the business and the team, not just the engineering.

### — A · De-risk: the dossier funnel —

> **Free / £1k / £5k are ONE document, not three** (full spec: `DOSSIER-TIERS.md`). Free = the £1k report with the *specifics masked*; £1k = a toggle that un-masks; £5k = a human audit overlay on top.

### Stage 0 — Free concept report  ·  lead-gen · AI · ~£0 cost
- **The SAME document as the £1k, page-for-page** — but the **actionable specifics are masked** behind an inline *"🔒 Upgrade to the £1,000 report to reveal."* The founder sees the full engineering, **all the costs and totals**, and the *counts* (N parts, N suppliers, N experts) — just not *which*. Value of upgrading is unmistakable.
- **Masked:** each BoM line's item + supplier (the *costs* still show); supplier names; the expert roles + questions. **Shown:** everything else.

### Stage 1 — £1,000: the full report, unmasked  ·  ~pure margin · AI
- **A toggle** — flipping to £1k instantly **un-masks the same document**: the **named parts / BoM**, the **supplier shortlist**, and the **expert *roles* + the questions** (who you *should* speak to, by type — NOT named individuals; the named person is Nick's value at £5k).
- Same render, no regeneration → ~£0 marginal cost (why it's pure margin).

### Stage 2 — £5,000: expert-audited  ·  FF takes 30–40% (~£3–3.5k to experts = 2–3 experts, ~½ day each)
- **Not a new document — a human audit overlay.** Experts go through the £1k report **section-by-section, page-by-page** and mark it up (the *"teacher checking the homework"*); the **AI then incorporates every audit note and cleans it up** → the £5k report. The AI did the homework, the human checks it, the AI applies the corrections.
- Covers **engineering AND the business plan.** Experts get the **deterministic models/tools** to check the numbers.
- **Sourcing the experts:** Nick / FF find the right expert per engagement at first (effort-heavy); the **roster builds over time and effort drops.** **Named experts revealed here**; escrow access after.
- **Scope discipline:** an *audit*, not a redesign — that protects the margin.
- **Ends with a clear hand-off to the RFQ stage** (Stage 3): *"next, we take this to your suppliers and bring back real quotes."*

### — B · Stand up the company: downstream, where the money is —

### Stage 3 — Costing / RFQ  ·  founder-paid coordination fee (flat ~£500–1,500/round)
- FF coordinates the RFQs (the supplier-facing legwork) and returns real quotes. **Founder-paid; never a supplier kickback.** Per-deal effort at first; over time FF builds **regular supplier relationships and the effort drops.**

### Stage 4 — Recruitment consulting  ·  with Nicholas Ball + FF fractional executives
- Identify **who the venture should recruit**, and where useful **place FF fractional executives as interim / part-time team members** — they may convert to permanent, or just cover the early stage. This is the "fraction of the people" *before* there's money for full-time hires.

### Stage 5 — Funding  ·  the engine: prep fee + EQUITY (NOT a raise fee)
- Build the **investment deck + data room** — including the **market / customer / go-to-market case** and a **"who to hire" plan** — and match to a curated early-stage hardtech investor network (**forge-capital**). **Demand-side first:** ask investors what they'll fund, then originate to match.
- **Revenue (FCA-aware):** charge a **fixed fee for the *materials + preparation* (deck, model, data room)** — a service — **+ take a small EQUITY stake.** We are paid for *building the deck and getting them ready*, and for the equity upside — **not** a cash percentage of money raised (that's the placement-agent / financial-promotion line to avoid). Equity is justified by the in-kind de-risking (HAX logic). *Still get the structure checked — even introductions + equity have nuances.*

### Stage 6 — Initial hires  ·  post-funding · with Nicholas Ball · share of placement fees
- Once funding is in, execute the **permanent hires** from the Stage-5 plan; Nicholas Ball runs the search. Revenue = a share of placement fees (≈15–25% of first-year salary).

### Stage 7 — Build  ·  post-funding (prototype earlier only if already funded)
- **Build needs money, so build follows funding.** If the founder already has some cash, they can do a **prototype build → then raise** for the full build; otherwise it's **raise → build.**
- FF coordinates the build **direct through the RFQ'd suppliers** (the one who quoted usually builds — treat them as the systems integrator, vet their critical subs). Capture the margin a marketplace (Xometry ~35%, Fictiv 15–25%) would take; borrow their **QA choreography** (vetting → test-part gate → scorecards) so a non-engineer founder trusts the build. Marketplaces only for commodity parts.

*Sequence: 0→1→2 (de-risk) → 3 (cost) → 4 (team) → 5 (fund) → 6 (hire) → 7 (build). **Build always trails funding;** recruitment consulting + the hiring plan feed the raise.*

## 3b · Operational mechanics (the HOW)

**Three generated artifacts (not two):**
1. **Engineering Dossier** — does it work + how to build it (tiered free / £1k / £5k; spec in `DOSSIER-TIERS.md`).
2. **Business/Market Report** — market, customers, go-to-market, business plan (a *separate* generator, same tiering — §9).
3. **Investor Pitch Deck** — built FROM (1) + (2) + the hiring plan at the **funding stage**; the investor-facing artifact. AI-generated + human-polished. The **deck-prep fee** pays for it.

**£5k expert mark-up — on the WEB, not the PDF.** The report is generated from structured state, so corrections must map back to that, not be scribbles on a flat file.
- Each assigned expert gets a **token-scoped review link** (a share link, no full login — same pattern as the brief editor) to **only their sections**.
- Inline, per claim / number / section: **comment · suggest a correction · approve.** Asynchronous.
- A **compile step** feeds all the experts' notes to the AI → it folds in the corrections + cleans up → **regenerates** the £5k report (web + PDF). *The teacher marks the homework on the web; the AI applies the marks.*
- Reuses the brief-editor infrastructure (a web surface that reads/writes state and re-renders).

**RFQ flow (the £5k → Stage 3 hand-off).** The validated BoM + supplier shortlist already live in the state.
- The "Start the RFQ" CTA → **auto-generates an RFQ package per part / sub-system** (spec + quantity + drawing refs, pulled from the BoM) → **sends to the shortlisted suppliers** → collects responses → compiles a **quote comparison** → returns it to the founder.
- FF coordinates + chases (the founder-paid flat fee). FF-operated at first; tooling + regular suppliers as the bench builds (effort drops over time).

## 5. Positioning & messaging

- **"A deterministic engineering system — computed and verified, not an LLM guess."** Circuit Mind's core trust move, and you literally have the deterministic tool packets + ~34 verification gates to back it. The #1 trust lever for a buyer who can't check the maths.

- Be **transparent**: "AI-generated, expert-reviewed." Opacity is a trust/legal wound; transparency converts better.

## 6. What we are NOT / what we deliberately drop

- **Not a studio** — no co-founding, no IP grab, no 30–50% stake (would break the wedge).
- **Not an accelerator** — no cohort, no up-front equity, no residency.
- **Not an engineering-tool shop** — the editable engineering-tool-integration layer is **dropped** (an engineer's nice-to-have, not the founder's need; others are better placed to build it).
- Also dropped: supplier kickbacks; AI-vs-expert opacity; auto pipe-routing (a multi-year CAD trap); DWSIM-embed (use it offline as a validator only).
- The existing builder/module output is fine — the frontier is **buildability + the funnel**, not tool depth.

## 7. Moat + competitive map

**Moat:** the **forge-capital investor-data-loop** (every dossier improves investor matching + the deal pipeline) + the **founder-keeps-equity inversion**. The AI-dossier edge erodes as tools mature → **race to lock the build + fund rails and the data loop.**

**Landscape (2025-26 scans, cited in the old doc):**
- **Accelerators / studios** — HAX (~$250k for ~10% + Newark residency), Bolt, EF ($125k/8%), Antler (7–12%), Deep Science — **all dilute the founder.** Closest to fear: **HAX** (idea→build→fund; bolt on an AI front-end and it's most of the thesis *minus* keep-your-equity).
- **Design consultancies** — Cambridge Consultants, PA, TTP, Synapse, IDEO — $150–500/hr, $30–150k+/job, no equity/funding tie. Their **free scoping call** is the real top-of-funnel substitute to beat.
- **Build/RFQ marketplaces** — Xometry (~35% take), Fictiv (→MISUMI), Protolabs — the **build rail to sit on / route around, not compete with.**
- **AI design tools** — Circuit Mind, Zoo/AdamCAD, Leap71 — **point tools for engineers**; none span design+cost+BOM+suppliers for a non-engineer, none touch funding. Watch **AdamCAD** (cheap, YC) drifting down-market.

**Mechanics worth stealing:** EF's *"walk away owing nothing"* (risk-free to engage); HAX's *"in-kind-as-equity"* (the AI's £50–150k-for-£0 gap justifies the downstream equity slice without being "a services business"); the marketplaces' *QA choreography* (vetting → test-part → scorecards); Circuit Mind's *deterministic* trust framing.

## 8. Risks & how the model answers them

- **£5k vet margin** → keep it a *vet, not a rebuild* (2–3 experts, ½ day); the report's near-zero AI cost makes the free funnel viable (so the old "50 free dossiers per sale" worry doesn't bite — free is genuinely cheap).
- **Willingness-to-pay unproven, both sides** → test the founder (£1k/£5k) AND the investor ("would you fund this?") before scaling.
- **Credibility is HIGHER-stakes for a non-engineer buyer** (they can't vet) → bar = "no error an expert would laugh at"; fix the wind/CO₂ cost misses first; deterministic + expert-reviewed is the trust stack.
- **FCA on funding fees** → equity-for-services, not cash success fees, until checked.
- **Moat erosion as AI tools mature** → speed to the data-loop + fund/build rails.
