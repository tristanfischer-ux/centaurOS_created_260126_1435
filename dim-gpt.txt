Here’s the concise, implementation-ready rubric I’d recommend for scoring **every section** of an engineering PDF.

## Recommended 6 Dimensions

### 1. **Clarity & Language**
- **Core question:** Is the section easy to read, precise, and unambiguous for its intended audience?
- **Why it matters:** Engineering documents fail when readers misinterpret wording, terminology, scope, or conclusions. Clear language reduces ambiguity, speeds review, and improves downstream decision-making.

### 2. **Structure & Presentation**
- **Core question:** Is the information organized and presented in a way that makes it easy to scan, follow, and extract key points?
- **Why it matters:** Even accurate content loses value if readers cannot find or interpret it quickly. Good structure, hierarchy, tables, visual layout, and formatting are essential for usability in long technical PDFs.

### 3. **Narrative & Logical Flow**
- **Core question:** Does the section tell a coherent story, with each part logically leading to the next and supporting the section’s purpose?
- **Why it matters:** Engineering sections should not be a pile of facts. They need clear reasoning: what the issue is, what was analyzed, what was found, and what follows from it. Strong flow improves comprehension and trust.

### 4. **Technical Accuracy & Fidelity**
- **Core question:** Is the content factually correct, technically sound, and faithful to the underlying data, calculations, assumptions, and source material?
- **Why it matters:** This is the baseline for engineering credibility. Incorrect numbers, faulty assumptions, or misrepresented evidence make a section actively harmful, regardless of how polished it looks.

### 5. **Completeness & Relevance**
- **Core question:** Does the section include all material information needed for its purpose, while excluding unnecessary or distracting content?
- **Why it matters:** A useful section must be complete enough to support decisions, but focused enough to avoid noise. Missing constraints, assumptions, risks, or context often cause more damage than poor writing.

### 6. **Decision Usefulness**
- **Core question:** Does this section help the reader understand what matters, what action to take, or what decision to make?
- **Why it matters:** This is the ultimate value test. Engineering reports exist to inform action—approve a design, understand cost, manage risk, or choose next steps. A section can be well-written yet still fail if it is not practically useful.

---

## Why this set works
These six dimensions cleanly separate:
- **How it reads** → Clarity & Language
- **How it looks / is navigated** → Structure & Presentation
- **How it thinks** → Narrative & Logical Flow
- **Whether it is correct** → Technical Accuracy & Fidelity
- **Whether it has the right content** → Completeness & Relevance
- **Whether it actually helps** → Decision Usefulness

This gives you a rubric that answers the founder’s core question:  
**“Is this section actually delivering value for the reader and is it useful?”**
