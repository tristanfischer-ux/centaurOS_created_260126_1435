# Fractional Forge — Monetisation Roadmap

*From an 18-agent council (2 red-teamers → 11-seat plan council + 1 house plan → 4 judges → 1 synthesiser), run 2026-06-07 on Andrew Symes's critique + Nick Ball's talent-alignment model. ~779k tokens.*

---

## TL;DR

**Stop selling the report. Sell the accountable human and the curated, design-linked expert network the report routes to.** The AI dossier is the **free lead magnet**, the credibility demo and the routing brief — every pound of revenue sits one step downstream, in people. Both the sceptical customer (Andrew) and the headhunter (Nick) independently endorsed exactly this.

- **First paid pound: 2–3 weeks** — via an advisory day (£1,500) or a Go/No-Go diagnostic (£3,000–3,500). Neither needs a roster.
- **Honest 12-month gross: £165k–£210k**, front-weighted to advisory/diagnostics. *Not* venture scale in year one (Andrew is right). The venture question is year-3: does the roster reach 75+ vetted experts and 15–20 institutional licences?
- **The proof the customer can't argue with:** the engine's own OXCCU output refutes the design-as-product model — for SAF the design barely moves economics (hydrogen price + site + policy dominate; the headline sat *below its own hydrogen floor*). That finding becomes the flagship **Go/No-Go diagnostic** product.
- **Show the commodity parts free; withhold who can *build* it** (sharpened 2026-06-08). The buyable catalogue parts (pumps, valves, instruments, standard exchangers) are ~10–15% of total project value and the most commoditised slice — show them, for credibility. The brokerable value is the **fabricator / engineering-procurement-construction firm / expert who builds the bespoke ~75–85%**, plus the diagnostic. There is **no paid "named-parts" upgrade** — that rung is killed.

## 1. Red-team verdict — Andrew is right (with one correction)

**Strongly agree, high confidence.** The dossier is not, by itself, a business:
- **The engine refutes its own product.** Its own SAF conclusion is "the design barely decides viability." A tool that nails the cheap 5% and is silent on the decisive 95% (feedstock/site/policy) is a study, not a business.
- **Design is the most commoditised layer and AI drives its price to ~compute cost.** No moat; a depreciating asset in a deflating market.
- **The accuracy/liability gap is lethal here.** One 3–5× cost error (the engine has produced them: £3,233/kW wind, SAF below its H₂ floor) ends a relationship in a small UK community. A PDF carries no liability — which is exactly why it's worth little.
- **"Knowhow" is structurally unreachable by the engine.** Tacit, unpublished know-how (the weld that passes NDT on *this* alloy, the failure that only shows at month 8) is by construction *not* in any LLM training set. The gap Andrew names is a category the engine cannot enter — not a quality bug it can iterate away.
- **The customer wrote the business plan.** He rejected the report and endorsed (1) linking experts to the design and (2) "revenue is the people, not the report." Steelmanning Andrew *is* adopting the pivot.

**Where he overshoots:** "you only need technicians" — regulated, capital-intensive hard tech doesn't buy on a technician + a chatbot; it requires a **named, accountable, liability-bearing** signatory (the bank/insurer/board needs someone to underwrite the judgement). The value isn't "technicians instead of designers" — it's the **trusted accountable human + the hands**. That seam is exactly what Fractional Forge can monetise. The dossier is also under-priced by Andrew as a **lead magnet** (a shockingly good free design is the cheapest customer-acquisition tool in deep tech), and there's an unbuilt business in being the **map** to the right scarce people — which the dossier's per-sub-module "experts to speak to" section already seeds.

All four judges (revenue realist, moat skeptic, Tristan-fit realist, and Andrew-as-customer) ranked the **people / expert-brokerage** plans top. - Free dossier = lead magnet + routing brief (one per account, kill-switch on).
- **Go/No-Go Feasibility Diagnostic** — productise the "single variable that kills the project" (the OXCCU hydrogen-floor finding) as a fixed-fee decision memo.
- **Credentialled Expert Sign-Off** — "when the former head of engineering at X reviewed this, here's what they changed and why." Citable in a data room; unrepeatable by AI.
- **Commission on the buyer, never the expert** (keeps expert loyalty); **charged upfront, before the intro** (anti-disintermediation).
- **Roster before marketing** — do not promote any expert product until ≥15 vetted people are mapped to the target sub-modules.
- **Institutional buyers** (TTOs / ICURe / accelerators) — budgeted, repeat demand that tolerates the BOM accuracy gap because the value is the qualitative routing.
- **Expert-override log** ("6mm not 4mm") = the one genuine long-run data moat.

## 5. What to KILL

- The **self-serve expert marketplace** in year one (the moment the roster is publicly searchable, buyers go direct and the trust anchor evaporates). Keep it private + curated until volume makes disintermediation irrational.
- **Selling the dossier as a paid product** (races to compute cost, no moat, fatal accuracy gap). Free, one-per-account, kill-switch on.
- **The "named-BOM paid upgrade"** (selling the commodity parts list, killed 2026-06-08) — the catalogue-buyable parts are ~10% of project value and the most commoditised slice; show them free for credibility. The withhold-and-charge step is *who can make/supply the bespoke scope*, not the part numbers.
- **Supplier take-rates / % of capex / % of capital deployed** in year one (unenforceable on off-platform B2B; empty-marketplace trap).
- Promoting any expert product **before the roster ≥15** (one delivery failure = permanent reputation damage in a small community).
- The **48-hour diagnostic promise** (it breaks under load; promise 5 working days).
- **Parallel cold-starts** across founders/TTOs/VCs/experts in the first 60 days.
- Over-weighting the 30,000-company supplier graph as the moat (a 12–18 month head start capital can replicate, not a wall).
- Treating **Andrew as a referral pipeline** (he gave a qualified endorsement, not a commitment).

## 9. Refinements after the council (2026-06-07, updated 2026-06-08)

**Show the commodity parts; withhold who can *make* it.** Sharpened 2026-06-08 (Tristan). The named parts in the bill of materials split into two kinds, and only one is worth withholding:

- **Commodity, catalogue-buyable lines** (pumps, valves, instruments, drives, standard exchangers — the branded part numbers the engine pins): **SHOW them, free.** They are ~10–15% of total delivered project value and the most commoditised slice — the buyer can purchase them off a distributor regardless, so hiding them protects nothing while showing them is the cheapest credibility the dossier has. There is **no "named-BOM paid upgrade"**; selling a buyable parts list chases the smallest, weakest ~10%.
- **Those who can *make* it** — the fabricators / engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) firms who build the bespoke, made-to-order scope (the reactor, columns, custom skids, the integration), plus the scarce experts: **withhold the names.** ~75–85% of delivered project value sits in this bespoke-plus-EPC layer, and "who can actually build this" is judgement and relationships an LLM cannot supply. The free dossier states the *type + spec* of supplier/fabricator and the *type* of expert needed, plus "we'll source them." Tristan sources the suppliers/fabricators; Nick sources the experts. That introduction is the brokerable step.

**Fractional Forge brokers *into* EPC; it does not *do* EPC.** EPC for a process plant is a capital- and liability-heavy certified-delivery business (Wood, Worley, Technip, mid-tier fabricators) — not a thin-AI-plus-curated-experts firm. The capture is the routing layer: diagnostic → vetted introduction to the fabricator / EPC firm / expert → sign-off → fee on the introduction/placement (and, phase 3, escrow on platform flow). The dossier is the credibility artefact and the routing brief that identifies the bespoke scope worth brokering.

**"The money is only in EPC" is too narrow.** The highest-confidence near-term cash is the **Go/No-Go diagnostic** (the one variable that kills the project — for SAF, hydrogen price + policy), which is neither parts nor EPC. Expert placement and diligence are also non-EPC revenue. EPC/fabricator introduction is the *bigger but later* prize; the diagnostic is the *first* pound.

**RFQ service (the supplier engine) — on the bespoke/engineered scope.** Turn each engineered sub-module's requirements into a request-for-quote pack, send it to vetted/relevant suppliers and fabricators (the 30k-company DB + signed suppliers), return a real quote comparison. The value concentrates on the bespoke scope (the buyer can quote the commodity lines themselves). It pays **three ways**: (1) the customer pays for the run (saves weeks); (2) suppliers/fabricators pay for qualified RFQ access (lead-gen they want); (3) the real quotes flow back to fix the engine's BOM pricing + seed the price-data moat — fixing the council-flagged weak spot from real data. A compounding loop.

**The product ladder:** free dossier with commodity parts shown (lead magnet + routing brief) → Go/No-Go diagnostic → bespoke-scope sourcing + RFQ run (suppliers/fabricators we introduce) → expert sourcing / sign-off (Nick) → pre-deal team / institutional / VC diligence → escrow fee on platform GMV (phase 3, once the curated roster carries routine deal flow, via the existing Fractional Forge app escrow + commission).

**Independence guardrail (unchanged):** supplier money (RFQ access, sponsorship) never biases the *independent* technical recommendation or the paid diagnostic/sign-off; supplier-experts are disclosed as interested parties.
