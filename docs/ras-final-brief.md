# Yellowtail Kingfish Land-Based Marine Recirculating Aquaculture System — Tayinloan (£5,000,000 capex ceiling)

We are designing a land-based, fully recirculating marine aquaculture system (RAS) that grows yellowtail kingfish (Seriola lalandi) from 200 gram juveniles to 3.4 kilogram sashimi-grade harvest fish on a 360-day cycle, on a consented brownfield former flow-through fish-farm site at Tayinloan, Kintyre, Argyll, Scotland. A recirculating aquaculture system is not a fish-tank system; it is a continuously-operated water-treatment and life-support plant wrapped around circular fish-rearing tanks. The tanks are the visible part; the engineering problem is holding dissolved oxygen, total ammonia nitrogen, nitrite, carbon dioxide, suspended solids, pH, alkalinity, salinity, temperature and pathogen pressure inside safe limits while the fish are fed heavily every day. The plant recirculates 99.6 percent of its water (approximately 0.4 percent new-water make-up), turning the tank volume over about four times per hour at a 15-minute hydraulic retention time.

The single hardest continuous duty is thermal. Raw seawater from the beach wells is 12 degrees Celsius and borehole water is 8 degrees Celsius, but yellowtail kingfish require 26.4 degrees Celsius, so the plant must lift the make-up water and hold the entire recirculating inventory at 26.4 degrees Celsius against building-fabric and process heat losses in a Scottish marine climate, recovering heat wherever possible.

Capex ceiling: 5,000,000 pounds for the recirculating aquaculture system equipment, shipping and installation. The reference full-scale commercial unit produces 204 tonnes per year of harvested kingfish. The engineering question this dossier must answer is what can be built for 5,000,000 pounds: the largest internally-consistent yellowtail kingfish recirculating aquaculture system — its annual harvest tonnage, its rearing-tank count and volume, and the capacity of every water-treatment stage — whose installed cost does not exceed the ceiling. Where the full 204 tonne per year unit will not fit the ceiling, report the achievable tonnage and what was made smaller to meet the price.

Production targets:
- Production capacity: up to 204 tonnes per year of harvested yellowtail kingfish at the reference unit; size to the largest tonnage that fits the 5,000,000 pound ceiling.
- Harvest weight 3.4 kilograms; grow-out cycle 360 days from 200 grams; feed conversion ratio 1.37.
- Harvest stocking density 60 kilograms per cubic metre; reference total rearing-tank volume 3,340 cubic metres at full unit scale.

Process description (continuous recirculating loop, in flow order):
- Rear the kingfish in circular, self-cleaning tanks with dual Cornell-type drains: a small bottom-centre solids-rich draw carrying the settleable faeces and uneaten feed in about 5 to 20 percent of the flow, and a larger elevated side-wall draw carrying the clean main flow; tangential inlet jets generate the rotational tea-cup flow that sweeps solids to the centre.
- Remove settleable and suspended solids immediately on tank exit in rotary drum microscreen filters with a 40 to 60 micron screen and automatic backwash, protecting the downstream biofilter from organic overload.
- Oxidise total ammonia nitrogen to nitrate in a moving-bed biofilm reactor biofilter carrying high-specific-surface plastic media at a 50 to 70 percent fill ratio, sized on the daily feed load, holding total ammonia nitrogen at 0.5 to 1.5 milligrams per litre and nitrite at 0.5 to 1.5 milligrams per litre.
- Strip carbon dioxide to below 6 milligrams per litre in forced-draught counter-current packed-column degassers with a high air-to-water ratio.
- Re-oxygenate with pure oxygen through down-flow bubble contactors (oxygen cones) fed from on-site liquid oxygen and a pressure-swing-adsorption oxygen generator, sustaining high dissolved oxygen at 60 kilograms per cubic metre stocking density; provision approximately 0.5 kilograms of oxygen per kilogram of feed.
- Disinfect and polish the recirculating water with ultraviolet reactors at a validated dose, with optional ozone under oxidation-reduction-potential control and residual destruct, to control free-floating pathogens, fine colloidal organics and off-flavour compounds (geosmin and 2-methylisoborneol).
- Heat and hold the water at 26.4 degrees Celsius using heat pumps with heat recovery from the warm recirculating loop; dose sodium bicarbonate to replace the alkalinity consumed by nitrification (about 7.1 grams of alkalinity as calcium carbonate per gram of ammonia nitrogen oxidised) and hold pH at 7.4.
- Return the treated, heated, oxygenated water to the tanks through the tangential inlets; bleed the 0.4 percent make-up, and thicken, dewater and dispose of the concentrated solids and sludge stream.

Water-quality set points (yellowtail kingfish): temperature 26.4 degrees Celsius, pH 7.4, full-strength seawater salinity approximately 33 parts per thousand, total ammonia nitrogen 0.5 to 1.5 milligrams per litre, nitrite 0.5 to 1.5 milligrams per litre, carbon dioxide below 6 milligrams per litre, dissolved nitrogen below 100 percent saturation, dissolved oxygen at or above saturation at the tank inlet.

Target market: sashimi and sushi-grade yellowtail kingfish (Hamachi) for high-end United Kingdom food-service and retail, sold under a committed annual off-take agreement, as a sustainable land-based alternative to imported tuna and air-freighted kingfish. Site: Tayinloan, Argyll, Scotland; an 18-acre consented brownfield former flow-through farm with seawater beach wells and freshwater boreholes giving roughly 124 cubic metres per hour of sustainable make-up water, four existing sea outfall pipes extending about 170 metres into the Sound of Gigha, and a planned 5 megavolt-ampere, 6 megawatt renewable micro-grid; United Kingdom deployment under Marine Scotland consent.

Life-support and backup (mission-critical: at harvest density the fish can die within about 20 minutes of an oxygen or flow failure): duty and standby recirculation pumps with gravity-flow fall-back, emergency pure-oxygen solenoid diffusers in every tank that fail open on power loss, a backup electrical generator, a liquid-oxygen buffer sized for several hours, and a fail-safe control system with low-dissolved-oxygen, high-and-low water-level, pump-trip and oxygen-pressure alarms with auto-dialler escalation.
