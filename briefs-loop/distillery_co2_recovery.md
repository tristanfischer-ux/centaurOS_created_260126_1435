# Containerised Carbon-Dioxide Capture and Recovery Skid — Distillery Fermentation Off-Gas

We are designing a single-container, skid-mounted carbon-dioxide capture, purification and liquefaction system that recovers the near-pure carbon-dioxide off-gas from whisky-distillery fermentation, cleans it to food-and-beverage grade, liquefies it, and stores it on site for reuse and local resale. The plant takes a raw fermentation off-gas stream (approximately 99% carbon dioxide, saturated with water and carrying trace ethanol, aldehydes and sulphur compounds) and delivers liquid food-grade carbon dioxide to a vacuum-insulated storage tank. The headline capacity is a recovery of approximately 2 tonnes of carbon dioxide per day. The purpose is to displace bought-in bottled carbon dioxide and to turn a vented waste stream into a saleable product.

Target market: malt and grain whisky distilleries, breweries, and bio-ethanol producers across the United Kingdom and Europe that have a clean fermentation carbon-dioxide stream currently vented to atmosphere and want an on-site recovery unit with a payback against bought-in beverage-grade carbon dioxide.

System description:
- A gas collection and buffering stage draws the carbon dioxide off the fermentation washback headspace (intermittent flow, approximately 150 to 250 kilograms per hour during active fermentation across multiple vessels) into a low-pressure gas balloon or buffer holder that smooths the intermittent supply
- A foam-trap and pre-scrubber removes entrained liquid, foam and the bulk of the water and ethanol
- A booster compressor raises the gas pressure for purification and liquefaction
- A purification train removes the remaining water (desiccant dryer), ethanol and aldehydes (activated-carbon adsorption), and sulphur compounds (potassium-permanganate or catalytic guard bed) to reach beverage-grade specification
- A refrigeration and liquefaction stage condenses the purified carbon dioxide to liquid at approximately minus 30 degrees Celsius and 15 to 20 bar
- A vacuum-insulated storage tank holds the liquid product, with a vaporiser for draw-off
- A control and instrumentation system manages the intermittent feed, purity monitoring, and safety interlocks

Key constraints:
- Carbon-dioxide recovery capacity: approximately 2 tonnes per day (2,000 kilograms per day), from a peak feed of approximately 250 kilograms per hour
- Product purity: beverage grade, better than 99.9% carbon dioxide, meeting the International Society of Beverage Technologists carbon-dioxide quality guideline (ethanol, aldehydes, sulphur and total volatile hydrocarbons within beverage limits)
- Liquefaction: liquid carbon dioxide at approximately minus 30 degrees Celsius and 18 bar
- Liquid storage capacity: approximately 5 tonnes (a vacuum-insulated tank giving two to three days of buffer)
- Feed stream: approximately 99% carbon dioxide, water-saturated, trace ethanol, aldehydes and sulphur, near atmospheric pressure, intermittent
- Footprint: a single 20-foot ISO container or equivalent skid (external 6.06 metres by 2.44 metres), outdoor installation
- Electrical supply: 400 volt, three-phase, 50 hertz low-voltage site supply; the design must state the connected and average electrical load
- Operating environment: outdoor, Scottish climate, ambient minus 10 degrees Celsius to plus 30 degrees Celsius
- Cooling: closed-loop refrigeration; no process cooling water assumed available
- Design life: 20 years
- Annual production volume: 25 units per year
- Unit cost ceiling: £250,000 installed (ex-works skid plus installation, excluding the building and the grid connection)
- Primary objective: balanced — the lowest installed cost that meets the recovery capacity, purity and footprint targets

Cost and payback context (for engine calibration):
- The recovered product displaces bought-in beverage-grade carbon dioxide at approximately £250 per tonne delivered; at 2 tonnes per day and 330 operating days per year that is roughly 660 tonnes per year, about £165,000 per year of displaced purchases plus any local resale
- A containerised capture-and-recovery skid of this scale is typically in the £150,000 to £250,000 installed range
- The dossier should report the connected electrical load, the average running power, and the annual running cost so the payback against the £250 per tonne bought-in price is visible

Safety and regulatory:
- Carbon dioxide is an asphyxiant — area carbon-dioxide monitoring, alarms and ventilation are required
- Pressure Equipment Directive / Pressure Systems Safety Regulations for the compressor, liquefier and storage tank
- DSEAR assessment for the ethanol-bearing feed gas (flammable vapour in the pre-purification stages)
- Machinery Directive, Low Voltage Directive and Electromagnetic Compatibility Directive for the equipment
- The recovered product must meet the International Society of Beverage Technologists carbon-dioxide quality guideline and relevant food-safety requirements
- UKCA marking

Sub-modules expected: fermentation off-gas collection and foam trap, low-pressure gas buffer holder, booster compressor, desiccant drying, activated-carbon and guard-bed purification, refrigeration and liquefaction unit, vacuum-insulated liquid storage tank and vaporiser, carbon-dioxide gas-quality analyser and area safety monitoring, control and instrumentation, electrical distribution and auxiliary power, and the containerised skid enclosure with environmental protection.
