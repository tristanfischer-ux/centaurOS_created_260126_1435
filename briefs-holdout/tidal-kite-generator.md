# Tidal Kite Subsea Generator Brief

We are designing a utility-scale underwater tidal kite — a tethered, "flying" tidal stream energy converter that harvests kinetic energy from coastal tidal currents. The kite carries a wing, a control system and a nacelle housing a shrouded turbine and direct-drive generator. Tethered to a seabed foundation, the kite flies a continuous figure-of-eight trajectory across the tidal flow, multiplying the water velocity across its turbine several times over relative to the free stream. This lets it generate economically in tidal currents too slow for a seabed-fixed tidal turbine. The device is a fully submerged, autonomous marine generator that exports electricity to shore over a subsea cable.

Target market: UK and EU tidal-energy developers and utilities deploying tidal-stream arrays in coastal and inter-island channels (Scottish islands, Welsh coast, Channel Islands, Northern France) seeking dispatchable, predictable renewable generation that complements wind and solar. Single-device sale ex-works; arrays of multiple kites share a common subsea hub and export cable.

Key constraints:
- Rated electrical output: 500 kW at the generator terminals in a 1.6 m/s tidal current
- Cut-in current: 1.0 m/s; rated at 1.6 m/s; survival (parked/feathered) to 3.0 m/s peak spring-tide flow
- Wing span: 12 m carbon-composite hydrofoil wing (this is the flying wing, not a rotor)
- Tether length: 80-120 m high-strength synthetic tether to the seabed foundation
- Operating depth: device flies between 15 m and 40 m below mean sea level; deployable in 60-120 m water depth
- Capacity factor target: ≥ 35% at a representative high-energy UK tidal site (predictable, phase-shifted across sites)
- Turbine: shrouded axial-flow water turbine, ~1.6 m rotor, direct-drive permanent-magnet generator, full seawater immersion
- Power take-off and export: variable-speed generator → subsea power electronics → 3.3 kV subsea export to the array hub
- Maximum device mass: 11,000 kg dry (excluding foundation and ballast); neutrally-to-slightly-positively buoyant in operation
- Design life: 20 years marine service; 5-yearly retrieval for major maintenance; antifouling and cathodic protection for full immersion
- Operating environment: full seawater immersion, salinity ~35 g/L, water temperature 4-18 °C, biofouling and sediment-laden flow
- Unit cost ceiling: £2,600,000 ex-works for the complete kite device (wing, nacelle, turbine, generator, power take-off, control system, tether, buoyancy) — excludes seabed foundation, subsea export cable, installation and grid connection
- Annual production volume: 12 devices per year
- Jurisdiction: UK (Scotland), with EU array deployments

Safety and regulatory:
- UKCA and CE marking
- DNV-RP-0286 / DNV marine renewable energy device certification for tidal energy converters
- IEC TS 62600 series (marine energy — wave, tidal and other water current converters), including IEC TS 62600-2 (design requirements) and IEC TS 62600-10 (mooring systems)
- Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC for the rotating turbine and control actuators
- IEC 60092 / IEC 61892 for marine and submersible electrical installations
- Cathodic protection and corrosion design to DNV-RP-B401 for the fully-immersed structure
- Lloyd's Register or DNV structural certification of the tether and seabed attachment
- Marine licence and environmental impact assessment (Marine Scotland / MMO); marine-mammal and collision-risk assessment
- G99 Issue 6 (UK grid connection) at the onshore export point

Sub-modules expected: carbon-composite hydrofoil wing with control surfaces, sealed nacelle, shrouded axial water turbine, direct-drive permanent-magnet generator, subsea power take-off and variable-speed drive, buoyancy and ballast control, synthetic tether and wet-mateable electrical connector, seabed foundation interface, autonomous flight-control and trajectory system with depth/attitude sensing, antifouling and cathodic-protection system, condition-monitoring and retrieval interface, and the subsea export-cable termination.
