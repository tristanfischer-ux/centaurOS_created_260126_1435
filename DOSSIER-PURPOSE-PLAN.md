_2026-06-05. ## The reframe (what it is)
A **concept-stage engineering & procurement briefing for a founder and a small team.** It gives a non-specialist team a complete, numbers-backed picture of the system — what it's made of, how it's engineered, what it costs (±30% at this stage), and where the risks and open decisions lie — built from real first-principles calculations. It is a credible first stab and **explicitly the basis for the expert and supplier conversations that come next**: it surfaces what to quote, what to validate, and what to ask. It de-risks the early stage so a small team can move with confidence before engaging expensive specialists. It is **not** a final engineering package or a vendor-quoted cost, and it says so throughout.

This makes the honesty features the *purpose*, not caveats: ±30% ranges, RFQ flags, the cost build-up, the physics closures — these are the founder's action list for the expert conversations.

## Three moves

### 1 · Reframe the Executive Summary
Open with a plain, prominent statement of:
- **What this is** — a concept-stage engineering + cost design with real numbers and calculations.
- **What it's for** — to let a founding team understand every issue in designing + procuring this plant, and to be the basis for informed conversations with engineering specialists and equipment suppliers.
- **Honest status** — a strong first stab, ±30%, NOT a final design; the next step is expert validation + vendor quotes.
- **How to use it** — understand the system, frame the right questions, take confident decisions; a one-line "how to read this document" pointer to the new Table of Contents.

### 2 · Add a "Taking this forward" section — the founder's action list
A consolidated, actionable closing section (before the appendices) that pulls what the dossier already knows into a to-do list for the expert/supplier conversations:
- **Get vendor quotes for** — the RFQ lines (crystalliser, columns, small heat exchangers…), with what to specify for each.
- **Validate with an engineer** — the physics-critic flags, the contract closures that warn, the key design assumptions.
- **Decisions still open** — e.g. the second-sink cation source, 316-solid-vs-clad metallurgy, the brief inconsistencies we surfaced.
- **Questions to ask, by area** — process, mechanical/materials, cost, regulatory — the specific questions a supplier or specialist should be able to answer.
This directly embodies the framing: "the questions they need to be thinking about that suppliers can answer."

### 3 · Thread the honest positioning consistently
Make every honesty feature serve the same story:
- The **cost build-up** (material take-off + fabrication, ±30% — the cost-section rework) shows the founder *how* the numbers are made and where the uncertainty is — and a supplier can react to "1,400 kg 316L + fabrication ≈ £42k ±30%" far better than to a black-box figure.
- The **RFQ flags** = the procurement questions.
- The **closures + physics critic** = the engineering questions.
- The **"Concept Stage" footer + ±30% statements** = the honest status.

## How it connects to the cost rework (already agreed)
The cost-section change — make the BoM *right* via material take-off + fabrication, show the build-up, ±30%, instead of a wrong-BoM-plus-correction overlay — is part of the same vision. Right, transparent, supplier-reactable numbers are exactly what makes this a usable basis for the conversations.

## Sequencing
Build these together as one focused pass — exec-summary reframe + "Taking this forward" section + the cost build-up rework — then a clean run + re-render. One coherent change: turning the dossier into an honest founder's decision-and-conversation tool.
