1. The Idea — Why Glyphs?

Chinese characters encode meaning compositionally. 銅 (copper) is 金 (metal) + 同 (same/together). 鋁 (aluminium) is 金 + 呂 (two mouths, stacked). The metal radical 金 appears identically in both — a visual signal that these are material siblings.

ForgeOS Radical works the same way. A radical is an atomic concept. A character is a composition of radicals. Three maximally different products — a 3.5 MWh battery energy storage system, a 30 kW heat pump, and a consumer cinematography drone — when written in this symbolic language, share many of the same glyphs. That shared visual vocabulary is the claim: the same library of atoms describes all hardware products.

"If you can turn the radicals into symbols… three products described in paragraphs and be able to see the commonality in symbolic representation."

Each Chinese character below has an English annotation underneath it. Real Chinese characters are marked real. Glyphs that do not exist as standard Unicode characters are marked constructed with a note explaining the stroke logic.

5. Grammar Rules — Visual Encoding

Grammar rules fire when specific radical combinations meet physical conditions. Three key rules are shown below as inline diagrams.

Kirchhoff Current Law

KCL_NODE_BALANCE

At any electrical node, currents must sum to zero. Fires whenever ≥3 電 paths converge at a node.

Galvanic Corrosion

GALVANIC_ALUMINIUM_COPPER_CONTACT

Direct contact of 銅 (copper) and 鋁 (aluminium) in a marine environment triggers rapid galvanic corrosion. Rule fires and flags a WARN.

Voltage Derate

VOLTAGE_DERATE_80PCT

Any 電 component operated above 80% of its rated voltage must be flagged. Below 80% is safe operating margin (green zone).

7. What the Symbols Show

When the three product paragraphs are placed side by side and the glyphs are consistently coloured, three insights become unavoidable:

  1. 固 is the ground state of hardware. Solid state of matter appears in every single character across all three products. It is the universal substrate — every physical component is solid, always. In prose this is invisible because it goes without saying. In glyph form it stands out as the most-repeated symbol, which raises an interesting question about whether it belongs at the radical level at all, or is better modelled as a precondition.
  2. 矽 is the great equaliser. Silicon semiconductor function appears in the BESS BMS, the heat pump controller, and nine separate drone components — flight controller, BMS, ESC, GNSS, IMU, barometric sensor, compass, video radio, and remote ID. A 40-ft energy storage container and a sub-899g flying camera share the same silicon radical in more than half their component lines. This is not a coincidence; it reflects the fact that all modern hardware is already substantially a software system that happens to sit inside metal.
  3. 熱+流 is the thermal signature. The combination of thermal transfer and fluid flow appears in the BESS liquid cooling loop, the heat pump vapour-compression cycle and water circuit, and the drone ESC heatsink (thermal) and fan flow. The combination represents "managed heat movement" — a recurring engineering problem regardless of product domain.
  4. Product-specific radicals are thin. The drone needed only 碳纖 (composite fibre) as a new radical — one out of 19. The heat pump needed zero new radicals. BESS needed 12 from scratch, but that was the second product ever. By the third product the library was already 96% sufficient. The marginal cost of adding a new product decreases rapidly.
  5. The visual form reveals grammar opportunities. Once you can see that 銅 appears in both BESS (busbar) and heat pump (refrigerant lines) and drone (battery connector), the galvanic corrosion rule immediately suggests itself as a cross-product risk whenever 銅 and 鋁 share a module. In prose, this connection requires a human engineer to make the link. In the symbolic representation, it is spatially obvious.
The symbolic vocabulary does not make hardware engineering easier. It makes the shared structure of hardware engineering visible — and visibility is the first step to systematic automation.
