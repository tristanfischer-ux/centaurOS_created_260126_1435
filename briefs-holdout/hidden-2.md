# Wave-Energy Point Absorber Brief

We are designing a wave-energy point absorber — a floating buoy that converts the heave (vertical) motion of ocean waves into electricity. A buoyant float rides the waves and moves relative to a reference (a submerged reaction plate / spar), driving a power take-off that generates electricity. The device is slack-moored offshore and exports power to shore via a subsea cable. It is engineered to survive extreme storm seas while generating economically in the everyday wave climate.

Target market: UK and EU marine-energy developers and island/coastal utilities deploying wave-energy arrays in high-resource Atlantic-facing sites (north and west Scotland, south-west England, western Ireland, Portugal) seeking renewable generation that is complementary to wind and tidal. Single-device sale ex-works; arrays share a subsea hub and export cable.

Key constraints:
- Rated electrical output: 150 kW at the generator terminals in the rated sea state
- Resource: rated in a representative Atlantic wave climate (significant wave height ~2-3 m, energy period ~8-10 s); survival design to a 50-year storm sea state (significant wave height ~12 m)
- Capacity factor target: ≥ 25% at a high-resource UK Atlantic site
- Power take-off: direct-drive linear generator (or sealed hydraulic PTO) converting heave motion; full-spectrum control tuning to the incident sea
- Float and reaction body: buoyant float ~6 m diameter; submerged reaction plate / spar providing the heave reference
- Mooring: slack catenary mooring rated for the survival sea state; quick-disconnect for retrieval
- Export: variable output → onboard power electronics → 3.3 kV subsea export to the array hub
- Maximum device mass: 35,000 kg dry (float, PTO, reaction body, onboard electrical); excludes mooring and ballast
- Design life: 20 years marine service; survival without generation through the design storm; biofouling and cathodic protection for the splash and immersion zones
- Operating environment: surface and splash zone in open ocean, salinity ~35 g/L, severe wave loading and slamming, biofouling, marine corrosion
- Unit cost ceiling: £1,200,000 ex-works for the complete device (float, reaction body, power take-off, generator, power electronics, onboard controls) — excludes mooring spread, subsea export cable, installation and grid connection
- Annual production volume: 20 devices per year
- Jurisdiction: UK (Scotland), with EU array deployments

Safety and regulatory:
- UKCA and CE marking
- DNV-RP-0286 / DNV marine renewable energy device certification for wave energy converters
- IEC TS 62600 series (marine energy), including IEC TS 62600-2 (design requirements) and IEC TS 62600-10 (mooring systems)
- Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC for the power-take-off machinery
- IEC 60092 / IEC 61892 for marine electrical installations
- Cathodic protection and corrosion design to DNV-RP-B401 for the immersion and splash zones
- Structural certification of the float, reaction body and mooring to the survival sea state (DNV / Lloyd's Register)
- Marine licence and environmental impact assessment (Marine Scotland / MMO); navigation marking and collision-risk assessment
- G99 Issue 6 (UK grid connection) at the onshore export point

Sub-modules expected: buoyant float structure, submerged reaction plate / spar, direct-drive linear-generator (or hydraulic) power take-off, onboard variable-speed power electronics, control and sea-state-tuning system, slack catenary mooring interface with quick-disconnect, cathodic-protection and antifouling system, condition-monitoring and retrieval interface, navigation marking, and the subsea export-cable termination.
