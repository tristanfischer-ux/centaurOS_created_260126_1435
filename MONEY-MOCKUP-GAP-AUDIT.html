3 · Red team 5 critiques

Sharp honest analysis of what's been built. These are the arguments that would come up in a design review — addressing them BEFORE code is cheaper than after.
1

We're building four products, not oneScope-creep critique

High
The Money section has become a CRM (Raise) + financial model (Plan) + bookkeeping integration (Xero) + pitch deck editor (Slides) + document tracker (Vault) + email marketing tool (Board pack sending). Each of those is a standalone product with a dedicated category leader. ForgeOS would be competing simultaneously with HubSpot, Carta, Xero, Pitch, DocSend, and Mailchimp.
Evidence from the mockups: 19 pages built, 47 additional gaps identified, total ≥ 66 surfaces before code. A specialist tool typically has 20-30 surfaces.

Why this happened

  • Every founder-facing question expanded to its own detail page rather than integrating into an existing surface
  • "The more we mock up the better" drove breadth rather than depth
  • The product vision is genuinely ambitious — one operational OS for hardware founders — but the Money section should not own the full fundraise CRM, pitch editor, and accounting system

What could break

  • Building all 66 surfaces to the quality bar = 6-12 months of engineering. Money is one of 5+ ForgeOS sections.
  • Each surface has a better specialist competitor. Founder dropout risk: "I'll use this for Cockpit but I have my real CRM in HubSpot."
  • Schema complexity balloons — investors, firms, touches, docs, pitches, slides, scenarios, line items, transactions all interconnect. Migration pain is real.
Mitigation (suggest):
• Ship MVP without: document vault, slide editor, theme editor, present mode, co-founder review threads, reply inbox, open-rate tracking. Defer to V2.
• Integrate rather than build: Gmail for touches (OAuth read), Google Slides for deck export (replace slide editor), DocSend for doc tracking (webhook only).
• MVP surface count: 12-14 pages. Ship Cockpit, Plan (grid + forecast + variance), Raise (kanban + pitch readiness + board pack), and 3 key drills (thesis, pipeline move, log touch). Everything else comes later based on real usage.
2

Most value depends on data the founder doesn't have yetCold-start critique

High
The mockups assume Xero is connected, Plan is populated, Strategy is written, Objectives are tracked, and the ForgeOS network has warm intros. A founder on day 1 has NONE of this. They see empty states nowhere in our 19 pages.
Evidence: Cockpit hero says "£312k · 26 weeks runway". Thesis Builder has "keyword fingerprint" pulled from docs. Board Pack auto-populates from Cockpit. Warm intro chips reference "via Max". Every number is populated — nobody has ever seen an empty Money section mocked.

What the founder actually does on day 1

  1. Lands on Cockpit → sees a stub — "Welcome to your Cockpit. Connect accounting or enter your first cost →"
  2. Opens Plan → blank grid with "Import from CSV" or "Start from template" or "Link Xero" as only CTAs
  3. Opens Raise → "Create your round" wizard before any pipeline exists
  4. Opens Thesis builder → manual 3-field fill (stage, sector, cheque range) since no Strategy exists yet

What could break

  • Founder signs up · opens Cockpit · sees something that doesn't match their reality · churns within 30 seconds
  • Empty states are often where product quality is weakest — every mockup shows the end state, not the journey
  • Onboarding has no mockup. That's a ~4-6 page flow that MUST exist before code
Mitigation:
• Build the onboarding mockup — 6 pages covering: welcome, connect accounting (skip OK), add 1 plan line (starter templates: "UK pre-seed with 4 engineers"), set thesis (3 questions), set round (optional), first cockpit tour
• Build empty-state mockups for Cockpit, Plan, Raise (at minimum) — these are the first impressions
• Time-boxed starter data: the first 7 days, Finn/Fiona help the founder populate — guided rather than empty
3

Specialist callouts are AI-branded — violates your own CLAUDE.md ruleCopy-voice critique

Medium
The mockups lean heavily on "Finn / Fiona / Harper pre-briefs" as UX primitives. The CLAUDE.md rule says: "No AI-powered", "AI-generated", "Smart", "Intelligent" labels · Test: Would this description make sense without mentioning AI? The specialist callouts fail this test — each one IS the AI agent.
Evidence: "Fiona · your fundraising lead: I've drafted the outline." · "Finn · your finance lead: I've done this automatically." · "Harper · HR: I've drafted both [payroll scenarios]." These cards explicitly highlight AI-driven work as the specialist's contribution. They read as AI-agent branding.

Secondary issue — voice drift

  • Silicon Valley cadence: "Two things to watch", "Highest-leverage move this week", "Killer feature".
  • Not Tristan's voice: British, understated, specific numbers, one clear next action. The rule from global CLAUDE.md.
  • Risk: when we build this in production, the LLM will generate copy matching our mockups' Silicon Valley voice — not Tristan's. Baking the wrong voice into 13 specialists is expensive to unwind.
Mitigation:
• Rewrite specialist callouts: remove the anthropomorphic framing ("I've drafted"), swap for direct imperatives ("Draft financial model · 3hr estimate"). Specialist avatar stays; "I did X" language goes.
• Frame specialists as roles, not characters: "Finance · Next action" rather than "Finn · your finance lead". Still useful for routing; less AI-branded.
• Voice sample: before writing production copy, pull 3-5 of Tristan's LinkedIn posts and investor emails as reference. Rewrite 5 existing specialist callouts in that voice. If they feel right, that's the style guide for the whole section.
4

The data model is underspecified — engineering notes are prose, not schemaTech-readiness critique

High
Each drill page has an "engineering note" at the bottom, but they are written as casual English. "Scenario schema: {name, question, visibility, ...}" is not the same as a migration SQL file or a TypeScript interface. When we start coding, we'll rediscover ambiguities that should have been resolved here.
Specific ambiguities I notice right now:
  • Source of truth: when Xero says payroll was £34.2k and Plan says £34.0k, which wins on Cockpit? Mockups show Xero-connected Cockpit and manual-entry Plan as if they're the same surface.
  • Scenario overrides: stored as diffs (flexible, recursive) or as full copies (simple, redundant)? Mockup says diff, doesn't specify conflict handling if base line item is deleted while scenario references it.
  • Pipeline events: described as an event log (good), but what's the consistency model? If two users move the same investor simultaneously, last-write-wins? Event-sourced? Mockup implies the former, schema should confirm.
  • Thesis versioning: mockup shows v3 history. Is each scoring run against the current thesis or the version at touch-time? Affects re-ranking semantics.
  • RLS: ForgeOS multi-tenancy is foundry_id-scoped. Every new table needs RLS policy spelled out. Zero mockups discuss this.

What could break

  • Wasted engineering time — discovering schema issues mid-implementation is 5x slower than before.
  • Migration pain — shipping v1 with the wrong shape means painful v2 migration.
  • Security gaps — RLS is easy to miss; foundry-isolation bugs are the worst kind in multi-tenant SaaS.
Mitigation:
• Before coding, write a proper SCHEMA.md: one markdown file per table covering columns, types, FKs, indexes, RLS policy, event log membership, soft-delete or hard-delete, versioning. ~15 tables for Money. 2-3 days of work. Pays for itself in a week of coding.
• Resolve the 5 specific ambiguities above — each deserves a 2-paragraph decision note.
• Write server action signatures: every action in src/actions/money-*.ts gets a typed signature before implementation. Prevents "what does this return?" loops.
5

We're over-engineering scenarios and under-engineering closeFeature balance critique

Medium
Scenarios have become a Monte Carlo simulation engine (formulas, probability weights, triggered actions, versions, diff-based storage) — but the actual fundraise close has no mockup at all. The moment a SAFE is signed and money wires in is the most important event in a founder's year, and we haven't mocked it.
Evidence: 3 scenario mockups (detail, new, wizard with 8 templates). 0 mockups for "SAFE signed · round closed · money wired · what happens next". The kanban has a "Closed" column with 1 investor (Founders Factory £180k) but there's no ritual, no status transition, no portfolio view.

Real founder usage

  • Most founders create 2-3 scenarios ever (default / bear / bull). A Monte Carlo engine is over-engineered.
  • Every founder goes through 1-3 round closes per year. That's where the emotional moment is. Where's the confetti?
  • Post-close, the investor becomes a portfolio relationship (updates, rights, info requests). We haven't modelled this.
Mitigation:
• Simplify scenario model: kill formulas-inside-scenarios (just overrides), kill trigger actions (defer), kill global parameters (defer). Scenario = set of line-level overrides. Ship basic, add when users ask.
• Add round-close flow: after moving an investor to Closed, 2-step close: (1) mark round closed (lock target, record actual), (2) convert close investors into portfolio-holders (ongoing updates, info rights, anti-dilution if applicable). Mock it.
• Portfolio view: one more surface — "your existing investors (portfolio)". Distinct from the pipeline. How they get their updates, voting, cap-table view. MUST-mock.
