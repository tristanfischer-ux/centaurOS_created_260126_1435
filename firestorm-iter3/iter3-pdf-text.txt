Overview
The UK R290 ASHP is an 8 kW residential air-source heat pump designed specifically for the UK market, utilizing low-GWP R290 (propane) refrigerant. It is engineered to integrate seamlessly with standard 200 L unvented hot water cylinders in semi-detached homes. The unit achieves a high Seasonal
Coefficient of Performance (SCOP) of 3.5 under average UK climate conditions, ensuring eligibility for
government grants like the Boiler Upgrade Scheme.

Mission
To deliver a highly efficient, low-GWP R290 heat pump tailored for UK semi-detached homes, fully
compliant with MCS and UKCA requirements, at a maximum OEM unit cost of £1,800.

Target customers
UK residential market, specifically semi-detached homes with 200L hot water cylinder integration. Installers seeking a streamlined, MCS-compliant R290 solution that is easy to fit and service.

Why now
Leveraging the founder's 12 years of experience at Mitsubishi Electric UK to meet the growing demand
for sustainable, low-GWP heating solutions driven by the F-gas phase-down and the UK Boiler Upgrade
Scheme.

Provides the rigid framework and acoustic attenuation to maintain the maximum mass below 90 kg
and sound power below 56 dBA. The chassis frame consists of a galvanized steel base pan and four
structural uprights that support the internal components. The acoustic enclosure wraps the compressor
in a mineral wool compressor sound jacket and seals the unit with a weather-resistant casing.
This passive containment ensures compliance with UK outdoor installation requirements. All external
panels are finished with a polyester powder coat rated to Qualicoat Class 2, providing UV stability and
corrosion resistance suitable for UK coastal environments. The internal layout segregates high-voltage
components from the refrigerant circuit using IP20 polycarbonate barriers, satisfying creepage and
clearance requirements under BS EN 60335-1.
Acoustic damping is further enhanced by bitumen-based mass-loaded vinyl sheets applied to the base
pan underside, reducing low-frequency resonance transmission to the mounting surface. Fastener access is standardised on M6 and M8 hex heads to simplify installer tooling requirements.

for freeze protection in exposed installations.

MODULE 6

Fan & Compressor Kinematics

Illustration only — AI-generated render. Module 6 (Fan & Compressor Kinematics) shown in identity colour; other modules muted;
enclosure ghosted.

Applies converted mechanical energy to move air and compress refrigerant. The air mover sub-module
consists of a 500 mm axial fan blade driven by the fan motor. The compressor mechanism utilises a
scroll compression set to physically compress the R290 gas.
These kinematic elements translate the electrical transduction into the physical fluid movement required for the 8 kW thermal cycle. The kinematic design prioritises low-vibration operation to extend
bearing life and minimise acoustic emissions. The fan motor is dynamically balanced to ISO 1940
G2.5 grade, and the axial blade is moulded with a scimitar profile to reduce tip vortex noise at higher
rotational speeds.
The compressor scroll mechanism utilises a compliant orbit design that allows the moving scroll to
separate slightly under liquid slugging conditions, preventing catastrophic mechanical failure during
abnormal operating events. Lubrication is provided by a centrifugal oil pump integrated into the crankshaft, ensuring consistent oil return to the bearing surfaces across the entire variable speed operating
range.

Risk & Failure-Mode Analysis
Class-level pre-mitigation hazards a heat pump design must address. Each hazard is rated on three 1-5
scales whose product gives a single risk priority number — higher means worse before mitigation.
Severity — how bad the outcome is if the hazard occurs (1 = inconvenience, 5 = injury / fire / total loss).
Likelihood — how often it tends to happen in fielded systems before mitigation (1 = very rare, 5 = frequent).
Detectability — how hard it is to spot before it causes harm (1 = obvious / instrumented, 5 = silent failure).
Risk priority — severity × likelihood × detectability. The single number used to rank hazards.

Heat pump hazards centre on refrigerant containment (especially A3-class refrigerants like R290 propane),
refrigerant-circuit pressure containment, and rotating-machinery safety. Residential R290 systems balance
flammability risk against the F-gas regulatory pressure to phase out high-GWP HFCs.
