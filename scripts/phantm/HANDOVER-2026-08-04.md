## 2. What is fixed, and what is still open

`femm/tooth_exact.py` corrects the decomposition: solve the tooth region (which
is genuinely prismatic, so 2D is exact there) with the return path drawn far away
and thick, and treat the real bridge reluctance separately.

It does **not** fully resolve. Our answer still depends on how far the return is
idealised, and depends most at small gaps where gap reluctance is least dominant:

| Return idealisation | 60 µm | 20 µm | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modest | 1.52 | 7.89 mN | 5.2× |
| Ideal limit | 2.34 | 13.90 mN | 5.9× |
| **Tony** | **4.16** | **6.86 mN** | **1.65×** |

**THE OPEN QUESTION: gap sensitivity, 1.65× (his) vs ~6× (ours).** Not a scale
error — a different curve. It is the number his manufacturing decision rests on.
Ideal unsaturated two-gap physics gives a ceiling of 9× (force ∝ 1/g²); fringing
can only reduce it, so ours is plausible and his is far below what the circuit
should give.

**Ruled out, do not re-run:** return-path distance and thickness (swept to the
ideal limit), pole slot depth (flat over 5×), translator slot depth (flat), MMF
scaling (fixes the mean, leaves 0.50–2.03 scatter), mesh and boundary.
