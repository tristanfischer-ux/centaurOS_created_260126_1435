Overview
This engineering decomposition details a 3.5 MWh Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) housed
within a standard 40-foot ISO container. The system is designed around 4,896 CATL 280 Ah LFP
prismatic cells, configured into 18 racks to provide 4.38 MWh of total energy, yielding 3.51 MWh of
usable energy at an 80% depth of discharge. A fully integrated 1 MW Power Conversion System
(PCS) steps the 870 V nominal DC bus to a 400 V AC output, enabling direct grid interaction. The
architecture emphasizes rapid deployability and high energy density while strictly adhering to a 28,000
kg maximum gross mass for standard road transport. Thermal management is handled by a 40 kW
liquid-cooling chiller loop, ensuring the LFP cells remain within their optimal operating temperature
range of -20 °C to +50 °C ambient. Safety is paramount, with NFPA 855 compliant Novec 1230 fire
suppression and deflagration venting integrated into the container shell. Control and communication
are centralized through an industrial Energy Management System (EMS) and SCADA gateway, provid-
ing the microsecond-level response times required for grid frequency regulation. The entire system is
factory-assembled, tested, and shipped as a single turnkey unit, minimizing on-site civil and electrical
works.


Mission
To provide a factory-assembled, rapidly deployable battery energy storage system for grid stability and
peak shaving applications, meeting a strict £180,000 ex-works cost ceiling and operational within 5
working days of delivery.


Target customers
UK grid-scale frequency response and capacity market operators. Commercial and industrial (C&I)
facilities requiring peak shaving and demand charge management.

MODULE 2

40 kW Liquid-Cooling Chiller
Rejects up to 40 kW of heat to the ambient environment to maintain the battery cells within their optimal
temperature range. The chiller compressor unit circulates 12 kg of R410A refrigerant to cool the liquid
loop via a brazed plate heat exchanger. A condenser fan assembly exhausts the extracted heat through
the container roof using variable-speed axial fans.

The HVAC air handler manages the ambient air temperature inside the PCS and control compart-
ments. Weather louvres protect the air intakes and exhausts from rain and debris ingress while allowing
adequate airflow. The environmental interface module delivers 40 kW cooling via a Copeland scroll
compressor circulating 12 kg R410A through a stainless steel 316L brazed plate heat exchanger.

Two 800 mm ebm-papst EC axial fans exhaust heat through the container roof while MERV 8 pleated
filters and condensate drip pans condition air for the PCS compartment. Weather louvres with extrud-
ed aluminum blades, stainless steel bird screens, and EPDM weatherstripping protect intakes from
ingress while maintaining airflow for 20 °C to +50 °C ambient operation. The thermal management
system utilizes a variable-speed scroll compressor that modulates cooling capacity between 10 kW
and 40 kW based on the battery heat load, minimizing parasitic power consumption during low C-rate
operation.

The brazed plate heat exchanger features asymmetric channel geometry to optimize heat transfer be-
tween the R410A refrigerant and the 30% glycol-water coolant loop, achieving a approach temperature
of 2 K at full load. Condenser fans are driven by EC motors with integrated PWM control, ramping speed
proportionally to the condensing pressure to maintain efficient operation across ambient temperatures
from -20 °C to +50 °C. The HVAC air handler supplies conditioned air to the PCS compartment at a
positive pressure of 15 Pa, preventing dust ingress through door seals while MERV 8 filters capture
particulates down to 3 microns.

Weather louvres are aerodynamically profiled to minimize pressure drop while providing 98% rain re-
jection at 15 m/s wind speeds, ensuring reliable airflow even during heavy storms.

MODULE 5

1 MW Power Conversion System
Converts the 870 V nominal DC bus voltage to 400 V AC at 50 Hz via a 1,000 kW continuous and
1,250 kW peak Power Conversion System (PCS). The PCS inverter module utilizes IGBTs and DC link
capacitors to synthesize the AC waveform from the battery DC supply. An AC filter network smooths
the switching harmonics using line reactors and filter capacitors before power reaches the grid.

The isolation transformer provides galvanic separation and steps the voltage to the required 400 V
AC output. The PCS control board manages the high-frequency switching and synchronizes with the
grid under the supervision of the site EMS. The energy conversion transduction module employs six
Infineon 1,700 V 1,000 A IGBT modules in a three-phase bridge topology, supported by twelve 1,000
µF 1,100 V DC link capacitors.

An LCL filter network comprising three 100 µH 1,500 A line reactors and six 200 µF 690 V AC capaci-
tors attenuates switching harmonics below IEEE 519 limits. The isolation transformer, rated Dyn11 with
silicon steel core and copper windings, steps the inverter output to 400 V AC while providing galvanic
isolation per IEC 62477-1. The PCS control board utilizes a Texas Instruments 200 MHz DSP and
50k-cell FPGA for microsecond-level grid synchronization and frequency response.

The power conversion topology utilizes a three-level neutral-point-clamped inverter architecture to min-
imize switching losses and reduce dv/dt stress on the motor windings and filter components. 25 MW
peak operation. Gate driver boards feature active short-circuit protection and desaturation detection,
shutting down the bridge within 2 µs of a fault event to prevent catastrophic module failure.

8 kHz, well above the 4 kHz switching frequency, with damping resistors absorbing harmonic energy
to meet IEEE 519 THD limits of less than 3%. The isolation transformer utilizes vacuum pressure im-
pregnated Class H insulation to withstand thermal cycling and moisture ingress, with integrated PT100
sensors in the windings feeding temperature data to the PCS control board for dynamic derating.

Risk & Failure-Mode Analysis
Class-level pre-mitigation hazards a battery energy storage system (bess) design must address. Each
hazard is rated on three 1-5 scales whose product gives a single risk priority number — higher means
worse before mitigation.


  Severity — how bad the outcome is if the hazard occurs (1 = inconvenience, 5 = injury / fire / total loss).
  Likelihood — how often it tends to happen in fielded systems before mitigation (1 = very rare, 5 = frequent).
  Detectability — how hard it is to spot before it causes harm (1 = obvious / instrumented, 5 = silent failure).
  Risk priority — severity × likelihood × detectability. The single number used to rank hazards.


BESS hazards are dominated by lithium-cell internal energy density and the thermal-electrical-chemical
coupling between cells. Single-cell failures (internal short, mechanical abuse) can propagate to module-level
thermal runaway within minutes if propagation barriers, off-gas venting, and suppression are inadequate.
