# Indoor Vertical Farm — Water, Fertigation and Ebb/Flow Irrigation System

We are designing the complete water-handling and nutrient-delivery plant for a multi-layer indoor vertical farm in the United Kingdom. The system takes United Kingdom potable mains water, purifies it, blends and doses it with nutrients to a controlled electrical-conductivity and pH, distributes it to thousands of flood-and-drain cultivation trays, collects the drain water, filters it and returns it for reuse. This is a process and fluid-handling plant — pumps, pipes, tanks, reverse osmosis, filtration, dosing and valves — for the irrigation half of the farm. The lighting, the climate and heating-ventilation hardware, and the building shell are explicitly out of scope.

Target market: indoor and vertical-farming operators, controlled-environment-agriculture growers, and large glasshouse growers across the United Kingdom and Europe who need a turnkey water, fertigation and irrigation plant for a multi-layer growing facility.

System description:
- A water-purification train treats the incoming mains water: a duplex water softener removes hardness, a granular-activated-carbon filter removes chlorine and organics, an 80-micron particle filter removes solids, and a reverse-osmosis unit produces purified permeate, with a controlled raw-water blend line to set the final feed-water conductivity
- A water-storage stage holds purified fresh water and collected drain water in galvanised tanks with per-tank level monitoring and suction lines
- A fertigation stage doses two independent nutrient streams (an A stream and a B stream) into the irrigation water through dosing units with venturi injectors, flow meters and inline electrical-conductivity and pH measurement, correcting to target with acid and nutrient dosing pumps and stock-solution tanks with mixers
- An ebb/flow (flood-and-drain) distribution network carries the dosed water from the fertigation room to every cultivation tray through mains, risers, electrically-actuated valve sections and distribution lines; trays flood to a shallow depth and drain back through a gravity drain network to a drain pit
- A drain-water recovery stage pumps the collected drain water through a cloth filter and returns it to the drain-water storage tanks for reuse, reducing fresh-water and nutrient consumption
- A separate hand-watering ring main provides manual watering points
- A process-control and instrumentation system manages the irrigation scheduling, the closed-loop electrical-conductivity and pH correction, level and flow monitoring, and the safety and fault alarms

Key constraints:
- Cultivation layout: two cultivation departments, each of 10 growing tunnels of 5 vertical layers (4 rows by 15 trays per layer), totalling approximately 6,000 ebb/flow cultivation trays of approximately 2,760 by 1,290 millimetres
- Irrigation duty: each tray floods to approximately 15 millimetres depth at approximately 1.5 cubic metres per hour inlet; trays are valve-grouped at approximately 30 trays per valve (approximately 200 electrically-actuated valves); maximum simultaneous irrigation demand approximately 45 cubic metres per hour per department
- Water purification: reverse-osmosis permeate capacity approximately 8 cubic metres per hour at approximately 75 percent recovery; softener and granular-activated-carbon throughput approximately 14 cubic metres per hour; a raw-water blend line of plus or minus 15 percent of permeate; target feed-water conductivity at or below 0.5 millisiemens per centimetre
- Water storage: one fresh-water tank and two drain-water tanks, each approximately 40 cubic metres
- Fertigation: two A/B nutrient-dosing units at approximately 45 cubic metres per hour each, each with a frequency-controlled circulation pump of approximately 7.5 kilowatts at approximately 3.5 bar, inline electrical-conductivity and pH measurement with closed-loop correction, acid and nutrient dosing pumps, and eight 1,000-litre nutrient stock tanks (four A, four B) with electric mixers
- Drain recovery: a drain-water collection pit of approximately 5,000 litres per cultivation room with a submersible transfer pump of approximately 45 cubic metres per hour, and a self-priming cloth filter of approximately 80 cubic metres per hour
- Hand watering: a frequency-controlled pump of approximately 25 cubic metres per hour at approximately 3 bar feeding a hand-watering ring main
- Electrical supply: 400 volt, three-phase, 50 hertz low-voltage site supply; the design must state the connected and the average electrical load
- Pipework: thermoplastic (polyvinyl-chloride and polyethylene) pressure pipework throughout, sized for the stated flows
- Design life: 20 years
- Annual production volume: 6 units per year
- Unit cost ceiling: approximately £1,250,000 installed for the water, storage, fertigation and irrigation plant (excluding the lighting, the climate and heating-ventilation hardware, the cultivation rack framework, the building, the main electrical switchboard and the cabling to the control points)
- Footprint: a fertigation and water-treatment plant room plus the in-house distribution network; indoor installation
- Primary objective: balanced — the lowest installed cost that meets the irrigation throughput, the water-purity and the layout targets

Cost and calibration context (for the engine):
- A real signed turnkey offer for a system of this exact scope (a two-department, 6,000-tray indoor farm) totalled approximately £1,250,000 to £1,400,000 installed, split roughly: water purification approximately £85,000, water storage approximately £31,000, fertiliser/dosing units approximately £92,000, the ebb/flow irrigation network approximately £895,000 (the dominant cost — it is overwhelmingly installed pipework, valves and labour), hand watering approximately £24,000, and the process-control computer approximately £128,000
- The ebb/flow irrigation network is by far the largest single cost, so the bill of materials and the cost build-up must capture the long pipe runs, the approximately 200 actuated valves, the risers and the drain network, not just the major equipment

Safety and regulatory:
- Pressure Systems Safety Regulations for any pressurised vessels and the reverse-osmosis high-pressure section
- Control of Substances Hazardous to Health assessment for the acid and concentrated-nutrient dosing chemicals (corrosive; segregated bunded storage, eyewash and personal protective equipment)
- Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations and appropriate backflow prevention on the mains connection (Fluid Category protection)
- Machinery Directive, Low Voltage Directive and Electromagnetic Compatibility Directive for the equipment
- Legionella / water-hygiene control on the stored and recirculated water
- UKCA marking

Sub-modules expected: mains water intake and backflow protection, water softening, granular-activated-carbon filtration, particle filtration, reverse-osmosis purification and blending, fresh-water and drain-water storage with level monitoring, A/B nutrient dosing and electrical-conductivity/pH correction, nutrient stock-tank storage and mixing, ebb/flow distribution mains, risers and actuated valve sections, gravity drain collection and drain pit, drain-water cloth filtration and return, hand-watering ring main, process-control and instrumentation, and electrical distribution and auxiliary power.
