# Containerised Battery Energy Storage System Brief — 20-foot, 5 MWh

We are designing the BATTERY container of a grid-tied lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery energy storage system: a single 20-foot ISO container holding the batteries ONLY. The headline configuration has a nameplate energy capacity of 5 MWh, and the power conversion is rated 2.5 MW maximum (PCS-limited). The 20-foot battery container is offered across an energy range of 5 MWh to 9 MWh (9 MWh long-duration coming soon) and a discharge duration family of two to eight hours, all at the same 2.5 MW maximum power: at 5 MWh it is a two-hour, 2.5 MW solution; a 9 MWh container is a longer-duration solution still capped at 2.5 MW. The system stores electricity from the grid and discharges it back on demand, providing frequency response, peak shaving and wholesale energy arbitrage.

IMPORTANT — SCOPE OF THIS CONTAINER: this 20-foot container holds the BATTERIES ONLY — the cells, modules, racks, battery management, liquid-cooling thermal management, fire detection and suppression, and the direct-current distribution and protection. The bidirectional power-conversion system (PCS), the step-up transformer, and the 11 kV / medium-voltage switchgear are NOT in this container — they are housed in a SEPARATE, co-located power-conversion container (a companion skid). The cost and footprint targets below are for the BATTERY container only (no power-conversion, no medium-voltage equipment).

Target market: UK and international commercial-and-industrial energy users, independent power producers and network operators seeking a high-density containerised storage block for frequency response (Dynamic Containment, Dynamic Moderation, Dynamic Regulation), peak shaving, and wholesale price arbitrage. Grid-tied deployment at 11 kV.

System description:
- High-density lithium iron phosphate (LFP) prismatic cells assembled into modules, modules into racks, and the racks installed in one 20-foot container (cutting-edge 2026 cell density, e.g. ~5 MWh in a 20-foot enclosure)
- The container holds the cells, module-level battery management, rack-level controls, and a liquid-cooling thermal-management system
- A bidirectional power conversion system (PCS) converts the battery direct current to grid-synchronised alternating current
- A step-up transformer raises the power-conversion output to the 11 kV connection voltage
- A site controller / energy-management system dispatches charge and discharge against grid frequency signals and market instructions
- Auxiliary systems: liquid cooling (chiller and coolant loop), fire detection and suppression, heating-ventilation for the electronics, and the metering and protection switchgear

Key constraints:
- Nameplate energy capacity: 5 MWh (5,000 kWh)
- Usable energy capacity: approximately 4.5 MWh at beginning of life (90% depth of discharge)
- Headline power rating: 2.5 MW (2,500 kW) continuous, two-hour discharge duration
- Duration family: configurable from two-hour (2.5 MW) to eight-hour (0.625 MW) discharge on the same 5 MWh container
- Battery chemistry: lithium iron phosphate (LFP)
- Direct-current bus voltage: approximately 1,500 V nominal
- Round-trip efficiency: at least 88% at beginning of life
- Cooling: liquid-cooled battery thermal management
- Grid connection: 11 kV, three-phase, 50 Hz, through a dedicated step-up transformer
- Grid code: compliant with Engineering Recommendation G99 for connection to the distribution network
- Footprint: a single 20-foot ISO container (external 6.06 m × 2.44 m × 2.59 m)
- Maximum gross mass: 44,000 kg — a 5 MWh 20-foot battery container is heavy-haul class (it exceeds the ~28-tonne standard road limit, as do all 5–9 MWh containers at current cell density), transported on a low-loader under a movement order rather than a standard trailer
- Operating environment: outdoor, ambient -20 °C to +45 °C
- Design life: 15 years, approximately 6,000 equivalent full cycles
- Annual production volume: 50 systems per year
- Primary objective: balanced — the lowest cost that meets the energy, power and grid-code targets

Cost anchors (industry pricing, 2026 LFP, for engine calibration):
- The battery-energy-storage portion only — cells, racks, module and rack battery management, liquid cooling, fire safety, controls and the 20-foot enclosure, but EXCLUDING the medium-voltage step-up transformer, the 11 kV switchgear, and civil works — costs approximately £63 per kWh (about $80 per kWh) at the two-hour configuration, falling to approximately £51 per kWh (about $65 per kWh) at the eight-hour configuration (longer duration amortises the power-conversion and balance-of-plant cost over more energy).
- For the 5 MWh container that is approximately £315,000 (battery-energy-storage only, two-hour) before the medium-voltage equipment and civil works are added.
- Unit cost ceiling: £900,000 ex-works for the complete containerised system including the power-conversion system and step-up transformer (excluding civil works and the grid connection).

Safety and regulatory:
- UKCA marking
- Engineering Recommendation G99 for connection to the distribution network
- IEC 62619 and IEC 62933 for industrial battery safety and grid energy storage
- NFPA 855 and UL 9540 / UL 9540A for energy-storage-system fire safety and thermal-runaway propagation testing
- BS EN 62477 for the power-conversion equipment
- DSEAR assessment for vented battery gases
- Machinery Directive, Low Voltage Directive and Electromagnetic Compatibility Directive for the equipment
- BS 7671 (IET Wiring Regulations) for the electrical installation

Sub-modules expected: LFP battery modules and racks, battery management system (cell, module and rack level), direct-current distribution and protection, bidirectional power conversion system (PCS), step-up transformer, 11 kV switchgear and protection, site energy-management controller, liquid-cooling thermal management (chiller and coolant loop), fire detection and suppression, heating-ventilation for the electronics enclosure, auxiliary low-voltage power and metering, and the 20-foot container enclosure with environmental protection.
