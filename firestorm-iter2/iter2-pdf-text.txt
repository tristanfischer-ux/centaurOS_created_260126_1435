Overview
This engineering decomposition details an 8 kW residential air-source heat pump (ASHP) optimised
for the UK market, utilizing low-GWP R290 (propane) refrigerant. Designed specifically for semi-detached homes, the system integrates with a 200 L domestic hot water cylinder to provide a complete,
low-carbon heating and hot water solution. The architecture balances high thermodynamic efficiency
with strict safety protocols required for A3 flammable refrigerants. The system is engineered to meet
a target SCOP of 3.5 under average UK climate conditions (BS EN 14825), delivering reliable performance from -25 °C to +45 °C ambient. To comply with MCS 020 planning requirements, the outdoor
unit features extensive acoustic damping to maintain sound power below 56 dBA. The design strictly
adheres to IEC 60335-2-40 Annex GG charge limits and BS EN 378-1 safety standards. Manufactured
at a target OEM unit cost of £1,800 and a maximum gross mass of 90 kg, the heat pump is positioned for high-volume deployment (5,000 units annually). It leverages variable-speed BLDC motors, a
brazed plate condenser, and an advanced inverter drive to maximise seasonal efficiency while ensuring
straightforward installation for MCS-certified professionals.

Mission
To deliver a highly efficient, compliant, and cost-effective low-carbon heating solution tailored for the
UK residential market.

MODULE 5

Compressor & Inverter Drive System

Illustration only — AI-generated render. Module 5 (Compressor & Inverter Drive System) shown in identity colour; other modules muted;
enclosure ghosted.

Converts electrical energy to mechanical work and transfers heat to deliver 8 kW of rated thermal
output at A7/W35 conditions. The power conversion system uses a 230 V AC inverter PCB to drive the
compressor and fan motors at variable speeds, drawing up to 2.28 kW of electrical power to achieve a
SCOP of 3.5. The compressor motor provides the mechanical torque for the R290 cycle, while the fan
and pump motors drive the external fluid loops.
Heat is exchanged via an 8 kW brazed plate condenser delivering heat to the water, and a finned tube
evaporator absorbing heat from the ambient air. The inverter drive incorporates a three-phase IGBT
power stage with integrated gate drivers and DC-link capacitors rated for 450 V DC bus. Heat from
the power electronics is dissipated via an aluminium heatsink with forced-air cooling from the main
condenser fan.
The scroll compressor uses a variable-speed BLDC motor with sensorless field-oriented control
firmware tuned specifically for R290 thermodynamic properties across the full -25 °C to +45 °C envelope.

Risk & Failure-Mode Analysis
Class-level pre-mitigation hazards a heat pump design must address. Each hazard is rated on three 1-5
scales whose product gives a single risk priority number — higher means worse before mitigation.
MANUAL REVIEW — G3 completeness
Review (G3) completeness gate exhausted retry budget — manual review required.

Severity — how bad the outcome is if the hazard occurs (1 = inconvenience, 5 = injury / fire / total loss).
Likelihood — how often it tends to happen in fielded systems before mitigation (1 = very rare, 5 = frequent).
Detectability — how hard it is to spot before it causes harm (1 = obvious / instrumented, 5 = silent failure).
Risk priority — severity × likelihood × detectability. The single number used to rank hazards.

Heat pump hazards centre on refrigerant containment (especially A3-class refrigerants like R290 propane),
refrigerant-circuit pressure containment, and rotating-machinery safety. Residential R290 systems balance
flammability risk against the F-gas regulatory pressure to phase out high-GWP HFCs.

massive voltage ripple and capacitor degradation.
