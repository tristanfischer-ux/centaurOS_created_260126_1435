# Wren Flyer — High-Altitude Platform Station (HAPS)

The Wren Flyer is Wren Aerospace's breakthrough high-altitude platform station
(HAPS): an unmanned, fixed-wing aircraft designed for long-endurance connectivity
missions in the stratosphere. It delivers persistent, regional high-altitude
connectivity from a fixed station-keeping orbit.

## Product
- Unmanned, fixed-wing HAPS operating in the lower stratosphere (approximately
  18-20 km altitude).
- Hydrogen and solar hybrid propulsion: wing-integrated photovoltaic arrays for
  daytime power and climb, and a hydrogen fuel-cell system for night-time flight
  and sustained station-keeping.
- Space-grade carbon-fibre composite airframe and a high-aspect-ratio wing structure
  with a 35-metre wingspan, optimised for minimum mass at high altitude.
- Proprietary autonomous flight-control and station-keeping system for persistent
  loiter over a fixed ground coverage area.

## Performance targets
- Wingspan: 35 metres (high-aspect-ratio composite wing for stratospheric lift).
- Payload: 50+ kg, a dedicated telecommunications / connectivity payload.
- Endurance: up to 9 days of continuous flight without landing.
- Coverage: 70 km ground radius from station-keeping altitude.

## Scope
- Design the complete aircraft: airframe and wing structure; the hybrid
  hydrogen-fuel-cell-plus-solar power and electric propulsion system; the avionics
  and proprietary control system; and the 50 kg payload bay.
- Stratospheric operating envelope: ambient temperature down to about -60 degrees C
  and ambient pressure around 5 kPa.
- Single-aircraft system; ground control and launch infrastructure are out of scope.
