# Test brief — physically impossible BESS

## Product
500 MWh battery energy storage system providing 1 MW continuous output for UK grid frequency response (G99 Issue 6, EFR market).

## Hard constraints
- **Envelope:** Must fit entirely inside a single 20-foot ISO shipping container (internal volume ~33 m³, internal dimensions 5.90 m × 2.35 m × 2.39 m).
- **Mass budget:** 1,500 kg total system mass, fully assembled.
- **Capex ceiling:** £50,000 ex-VAT, fully delivered.
- **Lead time:** 14 weeks ex-works.

## Performance targets
- Round-trip efficiency: 92% minimum.
- Cycle life: 6,000 cycles at 80% DoD, 25 °C.
- Operating temperature: −20 °C to +50 °C ambient.
- Compliance: IEC 62619, NFPA 855, UN 38.3.

## Reality check
At ~200 Wh/kg cutting-edge LFP cell density, 500 MWh requires ~2,500 tonnes of cells alone (1,667× the 1,500 kg budget). At typical pack-level density 150 Wh/L, 500 MWh requires ~3,333 m³ of pack volume (101× the 33 m³ envelope). At ~£90/kWh installed BESS cost (2026 market), 500 MWh installed = ~£45M (900× the £50K budget).

This brief is **physically impossible by three independent orders of magnitude**. A correct pipeline rejects it. A fiction-generating pipeline produces a passing PDF by inventing numbers.
